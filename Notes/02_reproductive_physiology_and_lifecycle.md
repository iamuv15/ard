## 2. Detailed Breakdown of the Oestrous Cycle

The oestrous cycle (standard 21 days $\pm$ 3 days) comprises a longer **luteal phase** (~16 days) and a shorter **follicular phase** (~5 days).

### A. Classical Four-Stage Breakdown
1. **Proestrum (2 days / 2–3 days):** 
   - Period of building up and growth of the **Graafian follicle**.
   - Fluid contains the hormone **oestrogen**, which induces changes in the uterus and increases blood supply.
2. **Oestrum (1 day / 12–18 hours):** 
   - The female is receptive and ready to receive the male.
   - Ovulation occurs **12 to 16 hours after the end of oestrus**.
3. **Metoestrum (4 days):** 
   - Implantation of the embryo takes place.
   - **Corpus luteum (CL)** forms, secreting systemic progesterone which regulates follicular development to medium-sized follicles and **prevents the growth of the Graafian follicle, thereby arresting the oestrous cycle** *(RRB SO-2018)*.
4. **Diestrum (14 days):** 
   - Further development of the uterus takes place. 
   - If the animal has not conceived, **involution of the uterus** occurs.

### B. Hormonal Regulation & Luteal Regression
* **Luteal Phase Dynamics:** Ovaries containing corpora lutea secrete systemic progesterone, blocking the onset of estrus.
* **Luteolysis:** At about **12–14 days** of the luteal phase, uterine production of **Prostaglandin $\text{F}_{2\alpha}$ ($\text{PGF}_{2\alpha}$)** causes regression of corpora lutea, resulting in declining systemic progesterone levels.
* **Gonadotropin Resumption:** Removal of the progesterone block permits secretory patterns of pituitary gonadotropins (**LH** and **FSH**), completing follicular development and estrogen production, triggering behavioral estrus.

## 3. Artificial Insemination (A.I.) & Semen Management

### A. Historical Milestones in India
* **First A.I. Attempt:** 1939 by **Dr. Sampath Kumar** at the Palace Dairy Farm, Mysore.
* **First Buffalo Calf via A.I.:** Born in **1943** at the Allahabad Agricultural Institute, Uttar Pradesh.

### B. Physiological Parameters & Viability
* **Vaginal Internal Temperature:** $40-45^\circ\text{C}$.
* **Sperm Viability in Female Tract:** Up to **24 hours**.
* **Ovum Fertile Life:** Only about **10 hours** after ovulation.

### C. Timing of Insemination (Cows)
* **Inseminated Same Day:** Cows showing oestrus in the **morning** should be inseminated the **same day (afternoon/evening)**.
* **Morning of Next Day / Early Afternoon:** Cows showing oestrus in the **afternoon** (or after 3 p.m.).
* **Too Late for Good Results:** Inseminating the morning of the next day if estrus was observed the previous morning.

### D. Cryopreservation Storage Temperatures
* **$-79^\circ\text{C}$**: Using solid $\text{CO}_2$ (dry ice) and alcohol.
* **$-196^\circ\text{C}$**: Using **liquid nitrogen** *(most commonly used)*.
* **$-296^\circ\text{C}$**: Using liquid helium.

## 5. Animal Breeding Systems & Classifications

### A. Primary Breeding Categories
* **Inbreeding / Close Breeding:** Mating closely related animals within the same breed (brother-sister, parent-offspring). Mates share common ancestors within **4 generations**.
  * *Advantage:* Maintains a pure line of a particular breed.
  * *Disadvantage:* **Inbreeding depression** (loss of vigor, reduced size, lower production, fertility problems).
* **Outbreeding:** Mating of unrelated animals of different breeds with **no common ancestor for a minimum of 4–6 generations**.

### B. Specialized Outbreeding & Crossing Systems
* **Out-crossing:** Mating animals within the same breed with **no common ancestors** on either side of their pedigree up to **4–6 generations**. 
  * *Application:* Best method for animals below average in milk production or beef growth rates; overcomes inbreeding depression with a single cross.
* **Top Crossing:** Use of **highly inbred males** on females of a base/non-inbred population (or continued use of superior sires across different families within a pure, same, or different breed).
* **Line Crossing:** Crossing of **inbred lines within a specific breed** to exploit homozygosity differences and capture **heterosis (hybrid vigor)**.
* **Line Breeding:** Mating of half-brother/half-sister or cousins to increase homozygosity and decrease heterozygosity *(AFO-2020)*.
* **Crossbreeding:** Superior males of one breed mated with superior females of another breed.
  * *Example:* **Hisardale** (sheep breed developed in Punjab by crossing **Marino rams $\times$ Bikaneri ewes**) *(RRB SO-2019)*.
  * *Cattle vs. Buffalo:* Crossbreeding is primarily used for genetic improvement of cattle; grading up is used for buffaloes.
* **Triple Cross / Rotational Crossing:** Crossing three breeds in a rotational manner. Males from pure breeds are used in alternate generations on crossbred females.
  * *Crisscrossing:* Rotational crossing between **two breeds** *(AFO-2021)*.
  * *Triple Crossing:* Rotational crossing among **three breeds**.
* **Back Cross:** Mating a crossbreed back to one of the pure parent lines *(JRF)*.
* **Test Crossing:** Breeding a dominant phenotype with a homozygous recessive phenotype (recessive parent/genotype) to determine zygosity.
* **Grading Up:** Mating purebred males of an established breed with non-descript females successively over generations. 
  * *Generational milestone:* After **7 generations**, non-descript females acquire full pure-breed characteristics.
  * *Example:* **Godavari breed of buffalo** developed by crossing local coastal Andhra Pradesh buffaloes with **Murrah** males over several generations *(RRB SO-2021)*.

### C. Interspecific Hybridization (Species Hybridization)
Mating male and female animals of two different related species to combine parental traits.

| Hybrid | Sire (Male) | Dam (Female) | Remarks / Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hinny** | Stallion (Horse) | Jennet (Donkey) | Inferior to mule as a work animal; **sterile**. |
| **Mule** | Donkey (Jack) | Mare (Horse) | Commercially important, highly valued work animal. |
| **Zebroid** | Zebra | Donkey / Horse | — |
| **Cattalo** | Cattle | Bison | Bison is the American buffalo. Males are **sterile**, females are **fertile**. Domestic bull $\times$ bison cow crosses show lower infant mortality because cow immune systems can reject hybrid calves. |
| **Cama** | Camel | Llama | Llama-camel hybrid; popular in the tropics, docile, better disease and heat resistance. |

### D. Assortative Mating
* **Positive Assortative Mating:** Mating phenotypically similar individuals (like with like, e.g., biggest with biggest, smallest with smallest).
* **Negative Assortative Mating:** Mating between dissimilar individuals (best to worst).

## 6. Swine Reproduction, Management & Farrowing

### A. Key Swine Reproductive Metrics
* **Age at Puberty / Gilt Breeding:** 8 months.
* **Weight at Breeding (Gilts):** 100–120 kg (Body weight should reach ~90 kg at 6 months).
* **Estrous Cycle:** 18–24 days (Average **21 days**).
* **Heat Period Length:** 2–3 days (Oestrus symptoms last 5–7 days with vulvar swelling and vaginal discharge).
* **Optimal Breeding Time:** Latter half of the first day for gilts; second day for sows.
* **Services per Sow:** **2 services** at an interval of **12–14 hours**.
* **Post-Weaning Heat Return:** 2–10 days. (Best results obtained by breeding at the **second post-lactational oestrus**).
* **Gestation Period:** **114 days** (Range: 112–115 days).
* **Litter Size:** Normal litter size is **8–10 piglets** (older sows yield larger litters with higher birth weights).
* **Boar-to-Sow Ratio:** **1 boar per 10 sows** for maximum fertility.

### B. Selection Standards for Replacements (Boars & Gilts)
* **Dam Litter Size:** Mother must have farrowed large litters of **8 or more piglets**.
* **Weaning Weight (at 56 days):** 
  * Gilts: Not less than **120 kg**.
  * Sows: Not less than **150 kg**.
* **Body Conformation:** Adequate length and depth, thick well-muscled hams (firm and trim), sound feet and legs.
* **Back Fat Thickness:** 
  * Gilts: **$\le$ 4 cm**.
  * Boars: **$\le$ 3.2 cm**.

### C. Farrowing Management & Piglet Care
* **Pen Preparation:** Clean and disinfect farrowing pens using **2% phenyl solution** and keep vacant for 1 week. Bed properly. Move clean animal into pen **10 days before farrowing**.
* **Parasite Control:** Deworm pregnant females **2–3 weeks before farrowing** (prior to pen entry). Spray with external parasiticide (**1% Malathion/Cythion** or **0.05% Butox**).
* **Needle Teeth:** Clip shortly after birth.
* **Iron Supplementation (Piglet Anemia Prevention):** 
  * *Injectable:* **50 mg iron** (Imferon 1 ml) intramuscularly on the **2nd day of life**. A second injection may be given at **5 weeks of age**.
  * *Oral Alternative:* 1 g Ferrous sulphate in 25 ml water (1 ml per piglet once a week).
* **Castration of Non-Selected Male Piglets:** Perform at **3–4 weeks of age** to prevent boar odor in cooked meat and ensure quality meat production.
* **Farrowing Duration:** Can last up to **24 hours**. Expulsion of litter takes **1 to 5 hours** (interval between piglets ranges from a few minutes to 3 hours).

### D. Lactation & Weaning (Swine)
* **Milk Production:** A sow yields about **150–200 kg of milk** during an **8-week suckling period** (sow milk is more concentrated than cow milk).
* **Feed Allowance:** 1.5 kg base feed for sow + 0.5 kg feed per piglet (maximum total ration: **5–6 kg**), supplemented with Lucerne hay and succulent fodder.
* **Weaning Age:** Usually **7–8 weeks** (56 days). Separate sow from piglets for a few hours daily to reduce stress; gradually reduce sow feed to dry the udder.
* **Wallows:** Mature breeding and fattening swine require masonry wallows with proper drainage during warm months due to a lack of functional sweat glands.

## 7. Camel Reproductive Physiology & Characteristics

* **Classification:** Pseudo-ruminant (ruminates, but possesses distinct polygastric morphological and physiological differences).
* **Dentition (Full Mouth - 34 teeth):**
  * Upper Jaw: 6 Incisors, 2 Canines, 2 Wolf teeth, 10 Premolars, 12 Molars = 34 total.
  * Lower Jaw: 6 Incisors, 2 Canines, 0 Wolf teeth, 10 Premolars, 12 Molars = 30 total *(Note: Text specifies total upper = 34, total lower = 34 based on raw text breakdown: IC W PM M = 6 1 6 / 6 1 8)*.
* **Oestrous Cycle (Rajasthan Camels):** **22 to 24 days** (general baseline: 10 days).
* **Heat Period:** **3 to 5 days**.
* **Gestation Period:** **365 to 400 days** (Average: **390 days** / text notes 391 days). First pregnancy is typically prolonged by 10–20 days.
* **Reproductive Frequency:** Gives birth to **1 calf** at a time, breeds **once in two years**, and suckles the offspring for **1 year**.
* **Abortion:** Common in Indian camels; primarily caused by **trypanosomiasis** or **scarcity of food**.
* **Common Infectious Disease:** 
  * *Anthrax (Local name: Sool)* — Causal organism: *Bacillus anthracis*. Causes sudden death without apparent cause.

> **Exam Trap:** Do not confuse *Yield Rate* rankings with *Percentage Share* rankings. Both hierarchies are heavily tested in ICAR and AFO examinations.
