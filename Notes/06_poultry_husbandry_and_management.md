## 1. Terminology & Nomenclature

| Term | Category / Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Group** | Flock |
| **Adult male (uncastrated)** | Cock / Rooster |
| **Adult female** | Hen |
| **Young male** | Cockerel |
| **Young female chicken** | Pullet |
| **New-born** | Chick (includes day-old chicks only) |
| **Roasters** | Mature chickens used for meat |

| **Layers** | Chickens developed to produce large numbers of eggs |
| **Laying** | Act of parturition in chickens |
| **Brood** | A group of chicks of the same age raised in one batch / hatched out from the same batch of eggs |

| **Brooder House** | A house used for rearing chicks from day-old with arrangements for artificial heating and light |

| **Moulting** | The process of shedding old feathers |
| **Culling** | Removing unwanted birds (sick, diseased, or useless) from the flock |
| **Debeaking / Beak Trimming** | Partial removal of the beak of poultry (especially layer hens and turkeys) to prevent cannibalism and feather pecking |
| **Cock** | A mature male chicken with coarse skin, toughened and darkened meat, and hardened breastbone tip |
| **Feeder (Feed Hopper)** | A specially designed trough or utensil where feed for chicken is kept |
| **Heavy Breeds** | Dual-purpose or meat-type breeds (e.g., Rhode Island Reds, Plymouth Rocks, New Hampshire) weighing at the age of sexual maturity $\ge 2.50\text{ kg}$ for females and $\ge 2.95\text{ kg}$ for cocks |

| **Layer House** | A house in which laying chickens are kept |
| **Laying Nest** | A box or cage where chickens lay eggs |
| **Perches or Roosts** | A circular or rectangular bar placed horizontally above the floor for chickens to roost at night |
| **Incubation** | Providing temperature and humidity for the normal development of an embryo in an egg (natural or artificial using an incubator) |
| **Hatching** | Process of breaking out of an eggshell after completion of incubation |

## 2. Biological Parameters & Management Standards

* **Chromosome number in chicken ($2n$):** 78
* **Incubation period:** 20 to 21 days (Standard: 21 days)
* **Age at sexual maturity:** 18–20 weeks
* **Incubation Temperature:** $37.2\text{–}37.7\ \degree\text{C}$
* **Hatching Temperature:** $37.5\text{–}37.8\ \degree\text{C}$ initially, then adjusted to $36.9\text{–}37.5\ \degree\text{C}$ after 19–21 days.

> [!WARNING]
> **EXAM TRAP: Debeaking Protocol**
> * Remove **only one-third (1/3)** of the upper beak.
> * Take extreme care to **avoid the tongue**.
> * Practice age: **10–14 days**, repeated at **14–16 weeks**.
> * **NEVER** use a penknife for debeaking.

## 5. Egg Composition & Grading

### Physical Composition
* **Average Egg Weight:** 56–57 g
* **Yolk:** 30%
* **White (Albumen):** 58%
* **Shell:** 12%

### Nutrient Composition (On Egg Weight Basis)
* **Protein:** 12%
* **Fat:** 11%
* **Minerals:** 12%
* **Water:** 65%
* **Calories:** $148\text{ kcal} / 100\text{ g}$

### Grading of Eggs by Weight

| Grade | Weight Range |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Extra-large** | $60\text{ g} / \text{egg}$ |
| **Large** | $53\text{–}59\text{ g}$ |
| **Medium** | $45\text{–}52\text{ g}$ |
| **Small** | $38\text{–}44\text{ g}$ |

### Grading of Eggs by AGMARK

| AGMARK Grade | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **A-grade** | Clean, unbroken shell; air cell 4 mm depth; clear, firm white; well-centered yolk free from defects. |
| **B-grade** | Clean, moderately tainted shell; air cell 8 mm depth; slightly off-centered and shape visible. |
| **No Grade / Loss** | Classified as loss or no grade (still edible if not spoiled, but contaminated by smoke, chemical, and other foreign materials affecting character and appearance). |

## 6. Systems of Poultry Rearing

1. **Free-Range System:** Common in rural areas using deforested or ordinary land.
   * Deforested land: **200 birds/acre**
   * Ordinary land: **100 birds/acre**
   * Most preferred system for **organic egg production**.
2. **Semi-Intensive System:** Night shelter provided with open, fenced runs for daytime use.
   * Stocking density: **6 to 200 birds per acre**.
   * Usually adopted for **duck rearing**.
3. **Intensive System:** Total confinement with internal feeding, watering, and nesting arrangements. Widely practiced in India. Subdivided into:
   * **Deep Litter System (RRB SO-2021):** Birds kept inside continuously on litter material (8 to 12 inches deep).
     * Materials: Paddy husk, sawdust, groundnut hulls, chopped paddy straw, wood shavings.
     * Seasonal depth adjustment: **10–12 inches in winter**, reduced to **2.5–4 inches in summer**.
   * **Slatted (Slotted) Floor System:** Iron rods or wooden reapers (2-inch diameter) placed 2–3 feet above ground level with **1-inch interspaces** for droppings to fall through.
   * **Slat-cum-Litter System:** Practiced for hatching egg production (especially meat-type breeders). **60% floor area on slats**, remaining **40% on litter**.
   * **Cage System:** Raised wire-netting compartments. **75% of commercial layers worldwide** are kept in cages.

> [!WARNING]
> **EXAM TRAP: Cage Layer Fatigue**
> * **Condition:** Lameness developing in caged laying birds.
> * **Potential Cause:** Calcium ($Ca$) and Phosphorus ($P$) deficiency, though the exact physiological reason remains undetermined.

## 7. Housing & Management Specifications

### Layer Housing Specifications
* **Floor Space:** $2.0\text{ sq. ft.}$ per bird
* **Feeder Space:** $4.0\text{ sq. ft.}$ per bird *(Note: Follow exact text parameters)*
* **Water Space:** $2.0\text{ sq. ft.}$ per bird
* **Required Temperature:** $10\text{–}24\ \degree\text{C}$

### Broiler Housing Specifications

| Parameter | 0–4 Weeks | 4–8 Weeks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Floor Space** | $\frac{1}{2}\text{ sq. ft.}$ | $1\text{ sq. ft.}$ |
| **Feeder Space** | $3\text{ sq. ft.}$ | $6\text{ sq. ft.}$ |
| **Water Space** | $2\text{ sq. ft.}$ | $4\text{ sq. ft.}$ |
| **Required Temperature** | $21\text{–}25\ \degree\text{C}$ | $21\text{–}25\ \degree\text{C}$ |

> [!TIP]
> **High-Yield Exam Focus (NABARD / ICAR / JRF):** 
> Memorize the precise broiler feeder space expansion (doubles from 3 sq. ft. to 6 sq. ft. at 4 weeks) and the exact litter depth seasonal variations (10-12" winter vs. 2.5-4" summer).
