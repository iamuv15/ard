## 1. Fundamentals of Soil Science, Approaches & National Institutes

### Definitions & Foundational Approaches
Soil science is the study of soil as a natural resource on the surface of the Earth, encompassing soil formation, classification, mapping, physical, chemical, biological, and fertility properties, and their management.

*   **Pedology** (Derived from Greek *pedon*, meaning soil or earth): Focuses on the origin, genesis, morphology, classification, and description of soil as a natural body in situ.
*   **Edaphology** (Derived from Greek *edaphos*, meaning soil or ground): Focuses on the study of soil from the standpoint of higher plants, emphasizing plant production, growth, and crop yield.
*   **Soil Fertility**: The inherent capacity of a soil to supply essential plant nutrients in adequate amounts and suitable proportions for plant growth **[UPCATET PG]**, **[BHU]**, **[AU PG]**, **[IFFCO AGT]**.
*   **Soil Productivity**: The capacity of a soil to produce a specific crop yield under a defined system of management (incorporates soil fertility plus climate, management, and physical constraints).

> *   **Father of Soil Erosion**: **H.B. Bennett**.

### Fundamental Physical Constants & Environmental Benchmarks
*   **Solar Constant**: The rate of solar radiation reaching the outer limit of the Earth's atmosphere is $\mathbf{2\text{ cal/cm}^2/\text{min}}$ ($1.361\text{ kW/m}^2$).
*   **Surface Tension of Water**: $\mathbf{72.7\text{ dyne/cm}}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$.
*   **Water Molecule Geometry**: Bond angle of $\mathbf{104.5^\circ}$ with a hexagonal lattice arrangement in the solid state (ice).
*   **Colloidal Size Threshold**: Soil colloids possess particle diameters of **$< 1\ \mu\text{m}\ (< 0.001\text{ mm})$** or $< 2\ \mu\text{m}$ for clay particles.
*   **Soil vs. Air Temperature**: Mean annual soil temperature is approximately $\mathbf{1^\circ\text{C}\text{ higher}}$ than the mean annual air temperature.
*   **Standard Temperature Measurement Depths**: International Meteorological Organization (IMO, founded 1873, restructured 1950) recommends standard depths of **10 cm, 20 cm, 50 cm, and 100 cm**.
*   **Redox Potential ($E_h$) Thresholds**:
    *   *Well-drained / well-aerated soil*: $\mathbf{+400\text{ to }+700\text{ mV}}$
    *   *Waterlogged / submerged soil*: $\mathbf{-250\text{ to }-300\text{ mV}}$
*   **Permissible Soil Loss Limit**: $\mathbf{12\text{ t/ha/year}}$ in India.
*   **Volumetric Conversion Benchmark**: $1\text{ ha-cm} = \mathbf{100,000\text{ Liters}} = 100\text{ m}^3$.

### Atmospheric Air vs. Soil Air Composition
Soil air occupies soil pores not filled with water. Its composition differs significantly from atmospheric air due to plant root respiration and microbial metabolism.

| Gas Constituent | Atmospheric Air (% by Vol) | Atmospheric Air (% by Wt) | Soil Air (% by Vol) | Key Pedagogical Note / Exam Distinction |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Nitrogen ($\text{N}_2$)** | **78.08%** | **76.50%** | **79.20%** | Slightly higher in soil air due to $\text{O}_2$ consumption. |
| **Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$)** | **20.97%** (or 20.9%) | **23.10%** | **20.60%** | Lower in soil air due to root/microbial respiration. |
| **Carbon Dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$)** | **0.033%** (or 0.03%) | **0.040%** | **0.30%** | **~10 times higher** in soil air than atmosphere. |
| **Argon / Others** | **0.93%** | **1.36%** | Variable | Minor inert trace gases. |

> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** Soil air $\text{CO}_2$ concentration ($0.30\%$) is **10 times (1000%) greater** than atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ concentration ($0.03\%$). Under severely waterlogged conditions, soil air $\text{CO}_2$ can exceed **1.0% to 5.0%**, creating anaerobic phytotoxicity.

The Earth's crust is composed of non-metallic (75%) and metallic (25%) elements:

*   **Structural Unit**: The **Silica Tetrahedron** ($\mathbf{[\text{SiO}_4]^{4-}}$) serves as the fundamental structural building block of all silicate minerals.

### Petrology: Classification & Formation of Rocks
**Petrology** (derived from Greek *petra* = rock, *logos* = science) is the study of rocks. **Petrogenesis** deals specifically with the origin and genesis of rocks **[HAU MSc Entrance]**. Rocks are hard masses of mineral matter comprising one or more rock-forming minerals, formed primarily from molten material known as **magma** (below Earth's surface) or **lava** (on Earth's surface).

```
Magma / Lava Consolidation ──> Igneous Rocks (Primary)
                                     │
                 Transportation & Cementation [JRF 2017]
                                     │
                                     ▼
                          Sedimentary Rocks (Secondary)
                                     │
                     Heat & Pressure Alteration [JRF 2018]
                                     │
                                     ▼
                             Metamorphic Rocks
```

#### 1. Igneous Rocks (Primary or Massive Rocks)
Formed by the cooling and consolidation of molten magma within (intrusive/plutonic) or on the surface (extrusive/volcanic) of the Earth.

*   **Granite**: Intrusive igneous rock consisting mainly of quartz, feldspar, and mica **[UPCATET PG]**, **[BHU]**, **[IBPS-AFO 2021]**.
*   **Gabbro**: Coarse-grained, intrusive basic igneous rock.
*   **Dolerite**: Intermediate intrusive igneous rock.
*   **Basalt**: Extrusive, fine-grained basic igneous rock (dominant parent material for Black Cotton Soils/Vertisols).
*   **Syenite**: Intrusive sub-acid igneous rock.

##### Igneous Rock Classification Based on Silica ($\text{SiO}_2$) Content:
1.  **Acid Rocks ($> 65\%\ \text{SiO}_2$)**: Granite, Rhyolite. High silica content; resistant to chemical weathering.
2.  **Intermediate Rocks ($56\text{--}65\%\ \text{SiO}_2$)**:
    *   *Sub-acid*: Syenite, Trachyte ($60\text{--}65\%\ \text{SiO}_2$).
    *   *Sub-basic*: Diorite, Andesite ($56\text{--}60\%\ \text{SiO}_2$).
3.  **Basic Rocks ($40\text{--}55\%\ \text{SiO}_2$)**: Gabbro, Basalt. High in $\text{Fe, Mg, Ca}$; weather rapidly.

> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** **Granite** is an **Intrusive Igneous Rock**, NOT a sedimentary rock **[IBPS-AFO 2021]**.

---

#### 2. Sedimentary Rocks (Secondary Rocks)
Formed through the weathering, transportation, deposition, and cementation (lithification) of pre-existing primary rocks **[JRF 2017]**.
*   **Sandstone**: Formed from cemented quartz sand grains.
*   **Shale**: Formed from consolidated clay and silt particles.
*   **Limestone**: Composed primarily of calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$).
*   **Dolomite**: Composed of calcium magnesium carbonate ($\text{CaMg}(\text{CO}_3)_2$).
*   **Conglomerate**: Formed from rounded gravels and pebbles cemented together.

---

#### 3. Metamorphic Rocks
Formed by the thermal, dynamic, or regional alteration of pre-existing primary (igneous) or secondary (sedimentary) rocks under extreme heat and pressure **[JRF 2018]**.

| Parent Rock | Metamorphic Equivalent | Rock Category of Parent |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Granite** | **Gneiss** | Igneous |
| **Basalt / Shale** | **Schist** | Igneous / Sedimentary |
| **Sandstone** | **Quartzite** | Sedimentary |
| **Shale** | **Slate** | Sedimentary |
| **Limestone** | **Marble** | Sedimentary |

## 4. Mineralogy & Weathering Resistance Series

### Mineral Classification
Minerals are naturally occurring, inorganic solids possessing a definite chemical composition and orderly internal crystalline structure.

*   **Primary Minerals**: Original constituents of igneous rocks (e.g., Quartz, Feldspars, Micas, Pyroxenes, Amphiboles).
*   **Secondary Minerals**: Formed from the chemical weathering and transformation of primary minerals (e.g., Clay minerals, Geothite, Hematite, Gibbsite).
*   **Essential Minerals**: Chief constituents of rocks that define the rock's fundamental character (e.g., Feldspars, Pyroxenes, Micas).
*   **Accessory Minerals**: Present in small quantities; their presence or absence does not alter the fundamental character of the rock (e.g., Tourmaline, Magnetite, Zircon).

### Goldich's Dissolution & Weathering Resistance Series
Minerals exhibit differential stability and susceptibility to chemical weathering.

| Resistance Class | Mineral Names | Composition / Key Attributes | Exam Relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Most Resistant** | **Quartz** | Tectosilicate ($\text{SiO}_2$); non-cleavable framework | Dominates coarse sand fraction |
| **High Resistance** | **Muscovite** | Potash white mica $\text{KAl}_2(\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10})(\text{OH})_2$ | Resistant due to strong bond architecture |
| **Moderate Resistance** | **Feldspars** | Orthoclase ($\text{K}$-feldspar), Plagioclase ($\text{Na/Ca}$-feldspar) | Moderate resistance; primary source of $\text{K, Ca, Na}$ |
| **Low Resistance** | **Biotite / Hornblende** | Black mica (Fe/Mg rich), Amphiboles | Rapidly weathered due to $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ oxidation |
| **Least Resistant** | **Olivine / Calcite** | Nesosilicate $(\text{Mg, Fe})_2\text{SiO}_4$ / Carbonate $\text{CaCO}_3$ | Dissolves readily in acidic water |

> 💡 **Exam Pointer:** 
> *   *Slowly Weathered*: Quartz, Tourmaline, Magnetite.
> *   *Moderately Weathered*: Apatite, Orthoclase, Muscovite.
> *   *Easily / Rapidly Weathered*: Olivine, Hornblende, Biotite, Calcite.

## 5. Clay Mineralogy, Soil Colloids & Cation/Anion Exchange

### Nature of Soil Colloids & Clay Minerals
Soil colloids are particle fractions with diameters **$< 0.002\text{ mm}$ ($< 2\ \mu\text{m}$)** that exhibit high specific surface area, electrical charge, and high chemical reactivity. Clay loam soil suitable for agricultural production typically contains **10–20% clay, 5–10% Organic Matter, and 30% Silt/Sand**.

#### Structural Typology of Crystalline Layer Silicate Clays:

```
                            ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                            │     LAYER SILICATE CLAYS     │
                            └──────────────┬───────────────┘
                                           │
         ┌─────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────┐
         ▼                                 ▼                                 ▼
  1:1 Type Layer                    2:1 Type Layer                    2:1:1 Type Layer
 (1 Silica : 1 Alumina)            (2 Silica : 1 Alumina)            (2:1 + Chlorite Sheet)
         │                                 │                                 │
  Kaolinite, Halloysite         ┌──────────┴──────────┐                   Chlorites
                                ▼                     ▼
                            Expanding            Non-Expanding
                        (Montmorillonite,       (Muscovite, Biotite,
                          Vermiculite)                Illite)
```

1.  **1:1-Type Non-Expanding Minerals**: Consists of 1 Silica tetrahedral sheet bonded to 1 Alumina octahedral sheet (e.g., **Kaolinite**, **Halloysite**).
