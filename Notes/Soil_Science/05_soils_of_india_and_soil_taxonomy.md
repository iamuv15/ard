## 1. Overview & Geographic Distribution of Soil Orders

The soils of India and the world are classified under the **USDA Comprehensive Soil Taxonomy** based on diagnostic horizons, moisture regimes, and soil-forming processes.

### Relative Area & Hierarchy of Soil Orders
* **Hierarchy in India:** **Inceptisols > Entisols > Alfisols > Vertisols > Aridisols > Ultisols > Mollisols > Others**
* **Hierarchy in the World:** **Aridisols > Alfisols > Inceptisols > Mollisols > Others**

| Soil Order | Area in India (%) | Diagnostic Characteristics / Key Features | Primary Indian Equivalent |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Inceptisols** | **39.74%** | Embryonic soils with weak horizon differentiation; minimum horizonation | Sub-recent Alluvial & Hilly soils |
| **Entisols** | **28.08%** | Mineral soils lacking distinct pedogenic horizons; recent origin | Fresh Alluvial (Khadar) & Diara land |
| **Alfisols** | **13.55%** | High-base status ($>35\%$ base saturation) with argillic ($B_t$) subsurface horizon | Red Soils |
| **Vertisols** | **8.52%** | High swelling-shrinking clays ($>30\%$ clay, rich in montmorillonite), slickensides | Black Cotton Soils (Regur) |
| **Aridisols** | **4.28%** | Soils of arid climates; gypsic, calcic, or salic horizons; low moisture | Desert & Saline-Alkali Soils |
| **Ultisols** | **2.51%** | Low-base status ($<35\%$ base saturation) with argillic horizon; highly leached | Lateritic & Acid Soils |
| **Mollisols** | **0.40%** | Soft, dark organic-rich topsoil with mollic epipedon; high base saturation | Terai & Grassland Soils |
| **Others** | **2.92%** | Histosols (organic), Oxisols, Spodosols, Gelisols, Andisols | Peaty, Marshy, Alpine Soils |
| **Total** | **100.0%** | — | — |

> 💡 **Exam Pointer:** 
> * **Inceptisols** cover the largest geographical area in India (**39.74%**), followed closely by **Entisols** (**28.08%**).
> * Globally, **Aridisols** occupy the maximum land area.

### 1. Alluvial Soils
Alluvial soils constitute the most extensive and agriculturally valuable soil group in India **[IBPS-AFO 2017]**.

* **Genesis & Mode of Formation:** Depositional soils formed by the erosion, transportation, and accumulation of sediments by river systems (Indus-Ganga-Brahmaputra plain, Narmada-Tapi plain, and deltaic regions) **[BHU PET 2016]**.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * **Texture:** Ranging from coarse sand to silty loam and heavy clay.
  * **Colour:** Light Grey to Ash Grey.
  * **Chemical Status:** Rich in Potash ($\text{K}_2\text{O}$) and Lime; deficient in **Nitrogen ($\text{N}$), Phosphorus ($\text{P}$), and Humus / Organic Matter**.
  * **Fertility:** Highly fertile; requires balanced chemical fertilization ($N$ and $P$) for optimal yield.
* **Sub-types (Khadar vs. Bhangar):**

| Parameter | Khadar (New Alluvium) | Bhangar (Old Alluvium) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Deposition Zone** | Low-lying floodplains, frequently inundated | Upper river terraces, above flood level |
| **Texture & Colour** | Sandy to sandy loam, light in colour | Clayey to clay loam, dark in colour |
| **Kankar Content** | Minimal to low nodular $\text{CaCO}_3$ | Abundant nodular impure $\text{CaCO}_3$ (Kankars) |
| **Horizonation** | Weak or absence of distinct soil horizons | Well-developed soil profile/horizons |
| **Fertility & OM** | Higher fertility, lower organic matter | Moderate fertility, higher organic matter |

* **Suitable Crops:** Rice, Wheat, Maize, Sugarcane, Cotton, Jute, Tobacco, and Oilseeds.

### 2. Red Soils
Occupying approximately **18.5%** of India's total geographical land area.

* **Genesis:** Formed *in situ* by the weathering and breakdown of crystalline igneous rocks (Granite, Gneiss) and metamorphic rocks under low to moderate rainfall semi-arid conditions **[BHU Pre PG 2012]**.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * **Soil Classification Name:** Also known as **"Early Soil"** because it warms up rapidly in spring, permitting early seasonal sowing.
  * **Colour:** Reddish due to the presence of **Ferric Oxide** ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$) in hydrated or crystalline forms. Lower horizons often transition to reddish-yellow or yellow.
  * **pH Range:** Acidic in reaction (**pH 4.5 – 6.5**). Soils derived from limestone parent materials have $\text{pH} > 5.5$, whereas those from shale/slate have $\text{pH} < 5.0$.
  * **Physical Behavior:** Porous, friable structure, well-drained with moderate permeability. Low Cation Exchange Capacity (**CEC**) and low Water Holding Capacity (**WHC**). Complete absence of lime nodules ($\text{CaCO}_3$ kankars).
  * **Nutrient Profile:** Highly deficient in **Nitrogen ($\text{N}$), Phosphorus ($\text{P}$), and Humus**.
* **Regional Variant:** Kerala possesses the highest frequency of yellow soil variants in India.
* **Suitable Crops:** Groundnut (**highly suitable**), Millets (Ragi, Jowar), Pulses, Sesame (Gingelly), Castor, and Tuber crops (Cassava).

> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** Do not confuse **Red Soils** (formed from Granite breakdown, acidic, Alfisols/Ultisols) with **Laterite Soils** (formed by extreme leaching of silica under high rain/temp, acidic, Oxisols/Ultisols).

### 3. Black Soils (Regur / Black Cotton Soils)
Occupying approximately **15%** of the country's geographical area.

* **Genesis:** Derived from the weathering of basaltic lava rocks across the Deccan Plateau.
* **Geographic Extent:** Maximum area in **Maharashtra**, followed by **Gujarat**, Madhya Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu. Deep black soils dominate the upper valleys of the Godavari and Krishna rivers **[MP FOREST SERVICES 2010]**.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * **Soil Classification Name:** Known as **"Late Soil"** or mature soil profile; locally termed **Regur** (from Telugu *Regada*, meaning cotton soil).
  * **Colour:** Deep black to light black/dark grey. The dark color is due to the **Clay-Humus Complex** and **Titaniferous Magnetite**.
  * **Texture & Mineralogy:** Heavy clay texture ($30–60\%$ clay fraction). Predominant clay mineral is **Montmorillonite** (2:1 expanding type lattice).
  * **Soil Reaction:** Neutral to alkaline (**pH 7.2 – 8.5**). Rich in Calcium Carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and Magnesium Carbonate ($\text{MgCO}_3$).
  * **Swelling & Shrinkage:** High coefficient of expansion and contraction. Swells substantially when wet and shrinks when dry, developing wide, deep vertical cracks ($> 1\text{ cm}$ wide up to $50\text{ cm}$ depth).
  * **Self-Ploughing Characteristic:** Dry surface fragments drop into vertical cracks during dry periods. Upon rewetting, the soil swells, creating internal pressure and soil inversion without mechanical tillage.
  * **Water & Air Dynamics:** Very high Water Holding Capacity (**WHC**), but extremely poor internal drainage and slow hydraulic conductivity.
  * **Nutrient Profile:** Poor in **Nitrogen ($\text{N}$)**, medium in **Phosphorus ($\text{P}$)**, medium to high in **Potassium ($\text{K}$)** **[Standard Indian Soil Characteristic]**.
* **Suitable Crops:** Cotton (primary indicator crop), Bengal gram (Chickpea), Mustard, Sunflower, Safflower, Sorghum, and Millets.

### 4. Laterite & Lateritic Soils
Covering approximately **12.1%** of India's geographical area.

```
High Temperature + High Rainfall
               │
               ▼
   Intense Leaching (Desilication)
               │
   ┌───────────┴───────────┐
   ▼                       ▼
Silica Leached Down    Fe & Al Oxides Accumulate
(Soluble Bases Lost)   (Sesquioxides Remain)
               │
               ▼
   LATERITE SOIL (Brick-like Hardness)
```

* **Genesis & Terminology:** Formed via **Laterization** (desilication) under hot, humid tropical climates with alternating wet and dry seasons. Derived from the Latin word *"Later"*, meaning **Brick**.
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * **Found In:** Mountain tops, hills, and foothill belts **[IBPS-AFO 2017]** (Western Ghats, Eastern Ghats, Rajmahal Hills, Karnataka, Kerala, TN, MP, Assam, and Odisha).
  * **Physical Consistency:** Soft and pliable when wet, but becomes **indurated and brick-hard upon drying**.
  * **Soil Reaction:** Moderately to strongly acidic (**pH 5.0 – 6.0**).
  * **Nutrient Profile:** 
    * **Rich in:** Iron ($\text{Fe}$) and Aluminum ($\text{Al}$) oxides (Sesquioxides).
    * **Deficient in:** Nitrogen ($\text{N}$), Potash ($\text{K}$), Phosphorus ($\text{P}$), Lime ($\text{Ca}$), and Organic Matter/Humus (high tropical soil temperatures accelerate microbial mineralization of humus).
* **Suitable Crops:** Acid-loving plantation crops (Tea, Coffee, Rubber, Pepper, Spices), Cashew nut (thrives on red laterites of Kerala, TN, and AP), Pineapple, and Avocado. Paddy is cultivated in lower elevated valleys.

### 5. Arid & Desert Soils
Covering ~**4.3%** of the country (primarily Rajasthan, Northern Gujarat, Southern Punjab, and Haryana).

* **Genesis:** Formed under arid and semi-arid conditions with high thermal regimes, sparse precipitation, and intense wind activities (Eolian deposits).
* **Physicochemical Properties:**
  * **Texture & Colour:** Coarse sandy texture, ranging from Red to Brown in colour.
  * **Soil Reaction:** Strongly alkaline with high pH and high total soluble salt (saline) content. In some saline hyper-arid pockets, common salt ($\text{NaCl}$) is commercially extracted by evaporating soil brine.
  * **Profile Dynamics:** Subsurface horizons ($B_k$ or $C_k$) exhibit heavy accumulation of **Kankar** (calcareous nodules) due to downward migration and precipitation of Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$).
  * **Infiltration Impact:** The dense subsurface *Kankar* band restricts deep percolation of water. When managed under micro-irrigation, moisture remains available in the root zone for extended periods.
  * **Nutrient Profile:** Deficient in **Nitrogen ($\text{N}$)** and organic matter; **Phosphate ($\text{P}$)** levels are normal to adequate.
* **Suitable Crops:** Drought-tolerant millets (Bajra, Ragi), Moth bean, Cluster bean (Guar), and Mustard (under irrigation).

---

### 6. Peaty & Marshy Soils (Histosols / Organic Soils)

* **Genesis:** Formed in areas of high rainfall, flooding, and high humidity, leading to waterlogging and anaerobic soil conditions. Anaerobic conditions inhibit microbial decomposition, causing massive accumulation of partially decomposed organic matter.
* **Organic Matter Classifications:**
  * **Peat Soil:** Contains **10–40% Organic Matter**; strongly acidic with **$\text{pH} < 4.0$** **[RRB SO 2018]**.
  * **Muck Soil:** Contains **20–50% Organic Matter** (highly/fully decomposed); **$\text{pH} > 4.0$**.

```
    PEAT SOIL                        MUCK SOIL
 ┌──────────────────────────┐     ┌──────────────────────────┐
 │ 10–40% Organic Matter    │     │ 20–50% Organic Matter    │
 │ Partially Decomposed     │     │ Well Decomposed          │
 │ Highly Acidic (pH < 4.0) │     │ Less Acidic (pH > 4.0)   │
 └──────────────────────────┘     └──────────────────────────┘
```

* **Local Nomenclature:** Known as **Kari** in the backwaters of Kerala **[NABARD 2019]**.
* **Physicochemical Properties & Specific Micronutrient Deficiencies:**
  * Heavy, dark black-coloured soils with high organic carbon content.
  * **Peaty Soils:** Substantially deficient in **Copper ($\text{Cu}$)**.
  * **Marshy Soils:** Substantially deficient in **Zinc ($\text{Zn}$)**.
* **Management & Use:** Submerged during monsoon; reclaimed post-monsoon for low-land Rice cultivation.

---

### 7. Special Regional Soil Types: Chalka & Forest Soils

#### A. Chalka Soils
* **Definition:** Red sandy loam soils covering extensive areas in **Andhra Pradesh and Telangana**.
* **Major Problem:** These soils become **extremely hard upon drying**, physically restricting seedling emergence and root growth.
* **Amelioration/Management:** Application and incorporation of slow-decomposing organic materials such as powdered groundnut shells, paddy husk, and coarse crop residues to improve aggregate stability and prevent crusting.

#### B. Forest & Hill Soils
* **Distribution:** High rainfall mountain zones (Himalayan belt, Western Ghats).
* **Properties:** Shallow depth, stony, highly acidic due to organic acid leaching, variable humus levels, lower crop suitability for field crops. Ideal for forestry, timber, tropical fruits, and fuel wood plantations.

## 4. USDA Comprehensive Soil Taxonomy Framework

The USDA system comprises **12 Soil Orders**, defined primarily by diagnostic epipedons (surface horizons) and endopedons (subsurface horizons).

```
                      12 USDA SOIL ORDERS
 ┌───────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┐
 │  1. Entisols  │  4. Vertisols │  7. Ultisols  │ 10. Histosols │
 │  2. Inceptisols│ 5. Aridisols  │  8. Oxisols   │ 11. Andisols  │
 │  3. Alfisols  │  6. Mollisols │  9. Spodosols │ 12. Gelisols  │
 └───────────────┴───────────────┴───────────────┴───────────────┘
```

### The 12 Soil Orders at a Glance

1. **Entisols:** Recent origin; no diagnostic horizons except ochric epipedon. Fluvents (alluvial), Psamments (sandy).
2. **Inceptisols:** Inception of horizons; weak subsurface development (cambic horizon $B_w$). Largest in India (**39.74%**).
3. **Alfisols:** Forest soils of humid/sub-humid zones; high base saturation ($>35\%$), argillic horizon ($B_t$).
4. **Vertisols:** High shrinking-swelling clay soils ($>30\%$ clay, montmorillonitic), slickensides, wedge-shaped structures.
5. **Aridisols:** Arid soil moisture regime; ochric epipedon, calcic, gypsic, or salic horizon.
6. **Mollisols:** Prairie/grassland soils; dark, organic-rich **mollic epipedon** ($>50\%$ base saturation).
7. **Ultisols:** Ultimate leached soils; low base saturation ($<35\%$), argillic ($B_t$) horizon, highly weathered.
8. **Spodosols:** Cold humid forest soils; **spodic horizon** ($B_{hs}$) with accumulated illuvial organic matter and $\text{Fe/Al}$ oxides.
9. **Oxisols:** Extremely weathered tropical soils; **oxic horizon** ($\text{Fe/Al}$ oxides), low activity clays.
10. **Histosols:** Organic soils containing $>20-30\%$ organic matter by weight ($>40\text{ cm}$ depth).
11. **Andisols:** Soils derived from volcanic ash; dominated by allophane and amorphous minerals.
12. **Gelisols:** Soils of frigid/permafrost regions; exhibit cryoturbation (frost churning).

## 5. Critical Soil Chemistry, Colloids & Mineral Summary

### Key Soil Science Laws & Formulas

#### Organic Matter Conversion Formula
$$\text{Organic Matter (\%)} = \text{Organic Carbon (\%)} \times \mathbf{1.724} \quad \mathbf{[AFO\ 2016]}$$
*(Where $1.724$ is the **Van Bemmelen Factor**, assuming soil organic matter contains $58\%$ carbon).*

#### Redox Potential ($E_h$) Limits
* **Well-aerated / Well-drained soils:** $+400\text{ mV to }+700\text{ mV}$
* **Waterlogged / Submerged soils:** $-250\text{ mV to }-300\text{ mV}$

#### Essential Plant Nutrient Absorption Forms
* **Nitrogen ($\text{N}$):** $\text{NH}_4^+$ (Ammonium), $\text{NO}_3^-$ (Nitrate)
* **Phosphorus ($\text{P}$):** $\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-$ (Primary Orthophosphate at $\text{pH} < 7.0$), $\text{HPO}_4^{2-}$ (Secondary Orthophosphate at $\text{pH} > 7.0$)
* **Boron ($\text{B}$):** $\text{H}_3\text{BO}_3$ (Un-ionized Boric Acid)
* **Iron ($\text{Fe}$):** $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ (Ferrous form)

## Previous Year Questions (PYQs) & Solved Rapid-Fire MCQs

### 1. Which soil order covers the maximum geographical area in India?
* **A)** Entisols
* **B)** Inceptisols
* **C)** Alfisols
* **D)** Vertisols

**Answer: B**  
**Rationale:** Inceptisols constitute the largest area in India (~**39.74%**), followed by Entisols (~**28.08%**).

---

### 2. The self-ploughing property and presence of wide vertical cracks upon drying are characteristic features of which soil? **[IBPS-AFO 2017]**
* **A)** Red soil
* **B)** Alluvial soil
* **C)** Black cotton soil (Vertisols)
* **D)** Laterite soil

**Answer: C**  
**Rationale:** Vertisols (Black cotton soils) contain high amounts of expanding 2:1 lattice clay minerals (Montmorillonite), causing swelling when wet and shrinking when dry, leading to deep cracking and self-ploughing.

---

### 3. "Kari" is the local name for peaty soils found in which state of India? **[NABARD 2019]**
* **A)** Tamil Nadu
* **B)** Andhra Pradesh
* **C)** Kerala
* **D)** West Bengal

**Answer: C**  
**Rationale:** Peaty and organic-rich saline soils occurring in the coastal/backwater regions of Kerala are locally termed **Kari**.

---

### 4. What is the standard Van Bemmelen factor used to convert soil Organic Carbon (OC) to Soil Organic Matter (SOM)? **[IBPS-AFO 2016]**
* **A)** 1.542
* **B)** 1.724
* **C)** 2.000
* **D)** 1.414

**Answer: B**  
**Rationale:** Organic matter contains approximately $58\%$ organic carbon. Thus, $100 / 58 = \mathbf{1.724}$.

---

### 5. Laterite soils are formed primarily by which pedogenic process? **[IBPS-AFO 2017]**
* **A)** Podzolization
* **B)** Gleization
* **C)** Laterization (Desilication)
* **D)** Calcification

**Answer: C**  
**Rationale:** Laterization involves intense leaching of silica and soluble bases under warm, humid conditions, leaving behind a residue rich in Iron and Aluminum oxides.
