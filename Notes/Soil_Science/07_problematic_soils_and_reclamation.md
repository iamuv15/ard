## 1. Overview & Classification of Problematic Soils

Problematic soils are soils whose physical, chemical, or biological properties constrain plant growth and crop productivity below their potential yields.

```
                   ┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
                   │       TYPES OF PROBLEMATIC SOILS       │
                   └───────────────────┬────────────────────┘
                                       │
        ┌──────────────────┬───────────┴───────────┬──────────────────┐
        ▼                  ▼                       ▼                  ▼
  ┌───────────┐      ┌───────────┐           ┌───────────┐      ┌───────────┐
  │ Physical  │      │ Chemical  │           │ Biological│      │Nutritional│
  │ Problem   │      │ Problem   │           │  Problem  │      │ Constraints│
  │   Soils   │      │   Soils   │           │   Soils   │      │(Secondary)│
  └───────────┘      └─────┬─────┘           └───────────┘      └───────────┘
                           │
       ┌───────────────────┼───────────────────┬───────────────────┐
       ▼                   ▼                   ▼                   ▼
┌──────────────┐    ┌──────────────┐    ┌──────────────┐    ┌──────────────┐
│ Saline Soils │    │ Sodic/Alkali │    │ Saline-Sodic │    │ Acidic Soils │
│(White Alkali)│    │ (Black Alkali│    │    Soils     │    │  (pH < 6.0)  │
└──────────────┘    └──────────────┘    └──────────────┘    └──────────────┘
```

### Core Categories of Soil Constraints
1. **Physical Problem Soils**: Highly permeable coarse sands, poorly permeable heavy clays, surface crusting, or dense hardpans.
2. **Chemical Problem Soils**: Saline, Sodic/Alkali, Saline-Sodic, Calcareous, and Acid soils.
3. **Biological Problem Soils**: Low microbial activity, organic matter depletion, or high soil-borne pathogen load.
4. **Nutritional Problem Soils**: Nutrient toxicities ($Al^{3+}, Fe^{2+}, Mn^{2+}$) or deficiencies ($P, Ca, Mg, Mo, B, Zn$) resulting directly from physical, chemical, or biological constraints.

## 2. Geographical Distribution of Salt-Affected Soils in India

Salt-affected soils predominate in **arid and semi-arid regions** where annual potential evapotranspiration exceeds annual precipitation.

### Distribution by Agro-Climatic Zones
* **Highest Area Occurrence**: Gujarat Plain and Hills Region $>$ East Coast Plains and Hills Region $>$ Upper Gangetic Plains Region.
* **Lowest Area Occurrence**: Eastern Plateau & Hills Region $>$ West Coast Plains and Hills Region $>$ Island Region.

### State-Wise Dominance & Exam Benchmarks
* **Highest Alkali/Sodic Soil Area**: Uttar Pradesh **[IBPS-AFO 2018, 2019]**.
* **Highest Total Salt-Affected Area**: Gujarat **[IBPS-AFO 2018]**.
* **Highest Vertisol (Black Cotton Soil) Area**: Maharashtra **[IBPS-AFO 2020]**.

## 3. Saline Soils (White Alkali / Solan Chalk / Reh / Thur)

### Genesis & Formation
* Formed primarily in **arid and semi-arid regions** receiving an annual rainfall **$< 55\text{ cm}$**.
* Capillary action draws dissolved salts to the surface during dry seasons. As water evaporates, a shimmering white salt crust accumulates on the surface.
* **Local Nomenclature**: Known as **Reh** or **Thur** in India, **Solan Chalk** globally, and historically as **White Alkali**.

> 💡 **Exam Pointer:** The white surface crust consists mainly of chloride ($\text{Cl}^-$) and sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$) salts of **Sodium ($\text{Na}^+$)** and **Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$)**.

### Diagnostic Criteria & Thresholds
* **Electrical Conductivity ($EC$)**: $> \mathbf{4.0\text{ dS/m}}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$ **[IBPS-AFO 2018, 2019]**.
* **Exchangeable Sodium Percentage ($ESP$)**: $< \mathbf{15\%}$ **[RPSC AAO 2009]**, **[MCAER PGCET]**, **[RRB SO 2020]**.
* **Soil pH**: $< \mathbf{8.5}$ **[RPSC AAO 2009]**, **[MCAER PGCET]**.
* **Soluble Salt Concentration**: $> \mathbf{0.1\%}$.

```
  Saline Soil Matrix:
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ High Soluble Salts (EC > 4.0 dS/m)                      │
  │ Low Exchangeable Sodium (ESP < 15%)                     │
  │ Flocculated Clays (Good Structural Stability/Permeable) │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** In saline soils, despite sufficient water presence in the root zone, plants experience "physiological drought" due to high osmotic pressure exerted by soluble salts, restricting root water uptake **[RRB SO 2020]**.

### Reclamation of Saline Soils
1. **Leaching / Flooding**: Flushing out soluble salts from the root zone using good quality irrigation water **[MCAER PGCET 2019]**.
2. **Subsurface Drainage**: Installing tile drains or perforated PVC pipes to permanently lower the saline water table.
3. **Application of Farmyard Manure (FYM)**: Improves soil structure, increases buffering capacity, and aids salt leaching.
4. **Salt-Tolerant Crop Selection**: Matching crops to root-zone salinity levels.

> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** **NEVER apply Gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$) to purely saline soils!** Gypsum adds sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$) ions, which further increases total soluble salt concentration and soil $EC$.

## 4. Alkali / Sodic Soils (Solonetz / Usar / Kallar)

### Genesis & Formation
* Dominant in regions with mean annual rainfall ranging between **55 cm and 90 cm**, particularly in low-lying topography with restricted drainage.
* Driven by excess carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$) and bicarbonate ($\text{HCO}_3^-$) salts of sodium.
* Hydrolysis of native calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) and exchangeable sodium releases hydroxyl ($\text{OH}^-$) ions:
$$\text{Ex-Na}^+ + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Ex-H}^+ + \text{Na}^+ + \text{OH}^-$$
$$\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{Na}^+ + \text{HCO}_3^- + \text{OH}^-$$
* **Local Nomenclature**: Termed **Usar** or **Kallar** in India, **Solonetz** globally, and historically **Black Alkali** (due to dissolved dark organic matter crusting the surface).

### Diagnostic Criteria & Thresholds
* **Electrical Conductivity ($EC$)**: $< \mathbf{4.0\text{ dS/m}}$ at $25^\circ\text{C}$.
* **Exchangeable Sodium Percentage ($ESP$)**: $> \mathbf{15\%}$ **[IBPS-AFO 2018]**.
* **Soil pH**: $> \mathbf{8.5}$ (typically ranges between **8.5 and 10.0**) **[IBPS-AFO 2015, 2016, 2017, 2018]**.
* **Soluble Salt Concentration**: $< \mathbf{0.1\%}$.
* **Physical Condition**: High $ESP$ causes clay hydration, leading to severe **deflocculation, dispersion, poor aeration, and hard surface crusts**.

### Chemical Reclamation of Sodic/Alkali Soils

Reclamation requires substituting absorbed exchangeable $\text{Na}^+$ on clay colloids with $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions, followed by flushing out the displaced sodium sulfate ($\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$).

```
           DISPLACEMENT REACTION IN SODIC SOILS:
 ┌──────────────────────┐                     ┌──────────────────────┐
 │ Soil  [Na+]          │ + CaSO4 (Gypsum) ──>│ Soil  [Ca2+]         │ + Na2SO4
 │ Colloid [Na+]        │                     │ Colloid              │ (Leached out)
 └──────────────────────┘                     └──────────────────────┘
```

#### Primary Chemical Amendments
1. **Gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**: Most widely used, economical, and direct calcium source **[IBPS-AFO 2021, 2022]**, **[MCAER PGCET]**.
   $$\text{2 Na}^+\text{-Clay} + \text{CaSO}_4 \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca}^{2+}\text{-Clay} + \text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4 \downarrow \text{(leached)}$$
2. **Iron Pyrites ($\text{FeS}_2$)**: Cheap chemical amendment containing ~22–24% Sulfur. Forms $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$ in soil, which dissolves native $\text{CaCO}_3$ **[IBPS-AFO 2021, 2022]**.
   $$2\text{FeS}_2 + 7\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{FeSO}_4 + 2\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$$
3. **Sulphuric Acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$)**: Fast-acting chemical agent; dissolves non-soluble calcite ($\text{CaCO}_3$) to yield soluble $\text{Ca}^{2+}$:
   $$\text{CaCO}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{SO}_4 \longrightarrow \text{CaSO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow$$
4. **Other Amendments**: Calcium chloride ($\text{CaCl}_2$), Ferrous sulphate ($\text{FeSO}_4$), and Aluminium sulphate ($\text{Al}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3$).

#### Biological & Agronomic Management
* **Green Manuring**: Incorporating *Sesbania aculeata* (Dhaincha) generates organic acids and $\text{CO}_2$, lowering soil pH and dissolving native $\text{CaCO}_3$.
* **Sodicity-Tolerant Crops**: Rice, Sugar beet, Dhaincha (*Sesbania*).

> 💡 **Exam Pointer:** **Calcareous Sodic Soils** contain free calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) with $EC \approx 1.74\text{ dS/m}$ and $pH > 8.5$. For these soils, **Sulphuric acid ($\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$)** is applied instead of Gypsum to dissolve native $\text{CaCO}_3$ **[MCAER PGCET]**.

| ESP Range (%) | Sodicity Tolerance Level | Representative Crops / Fruit Trees |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **2 – 10** | **Least Tolerant / Highly Sensitive** | Deciduous fruits, nuts, citrus, avocado, mango, jackfruit, banana **[RRB SO 2018, 2019]** |

## 5. Saline-Alkali Soils

Saline-alkali soils represent a transitional chemical condition exhibiting combined traits of both salinization and alkalization.

### Diagnostic Thresholds
* **Soil pH**: $> \mathbf{8.5}$ **[IBPS-AFO 2018]**.
* **Electrical Conductivity ($EC$)**: $> \mathbf{4.0\text{ dS/m}}$ **[IBPS-AFO 2018]**.
* **Exchangeable Sodium Percentage ($ESP$)**: $> \mathbf{15\%}$ **[IBPS-AFO 2018]**.

> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** If a saline-alkali soil is leached with pure water *without* adding calcium amendments (like Gypsum), the soluble salts are washed away while exchangeable $\text{Na}^+$ remains attached to colloids. This transforms the soil into a highly dispersed, pure **Sodic/Alkali Soil**!

## 6. Acid Soils & Soil Acidity

### Genesis & Characteristics
* Developed under **high rainfall / humid climates** where precipitation significantly exceeds evapotranspiration.
* Excessive leaching strips basic cations ($\text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{K}^+, \text{Na}^+$) from upper soil horizons, leaving behind acidic cations ($\text{H}^+$ and $\text{Al}^{3+}$).
* Primary parental rocks rich in **Granite** and minerals like **Quartz** yield acidic soils.
* **Geographical Distribution**:
  * **Highest Percentage Basis**: Kerala.
  * **Highest Total Area Basis**: West Bengal.

```
       HIGH PRECIPITATION (> Evapotranspiration)
                         │
                         ▼
      Leaching of Soluble Bases (Ca2+, Mg2+, K+)
                         │
                         ▼
   Dominance of Absorbed H+ and Al3+ on Soil Colloids
                         │
                         ▼
        SOIL ACIDITY DEVELOPMENT (pH < 6.0)
```

### The Three Pools of Soil Acidity
Soil acidity exists in three distinct equilibrium pools:

$$\mathbf{\text{Total Acidity}} = \mathbf{\text{Active Acidity}} + \mathbf{\text{Exchangeable Acidity}} + \mathbf{\text{Residual Acidity}}$$

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        POOLS OF SOIL ACIDITY                           │
├───────────────────┬───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┤
│  ACTIVE ACIDITY   │   EXCHANGEABLE ACIDITY    │    RESIDUAL ACIDITY    │
│  (Soil Solution)  │    (Colloidal Exchange)   │ (Non-Exchangeable SOM) │
│   H+ & Al3+ ions  │     Adsorbed H+ & Al3+    │ Al-hydroxyl complexes  │
└─────────┬─────────┴─────────────┬─────────────┴───────────┬────────────┘
          │                       │                         │
          └───────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Active Acidity**: Concentration of $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{Al}^{3+}$ ions present directly in the **soil solution** **[BHU MSc]**. Measured by standard pH electrodes.
2. **Exchangeable Acidity**: Ions of $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{Al}^{3+}$ adsorbed onto the surface of **clay and humus colloids**. Displaceable by unbuffered neutral salts (e.g., $\text{KCl}$).
3. **Residual Acidity**: Acidity associated with aluminum hydroxyl ions and $\text{H}^+ / \text{Al}^{3+}$ chemically bound in non-exchangeable forms to organic matter and silicate clays.

### Soil Acidity Classification Ratings

| pH Range | Acidity Rating | Exam Reference |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **3.0 – 4.0** | Very Strong Acidity | |
| **4.0 – 5.0** | Strong Acidity | |

| **6.0 – 7.0** | Slight Acidity | **[IBPS-AFO 2019]** |

### Nutritional & Biological Constraints in Acid Soils
1. **Elemental Toxicity**: High solubility and toxic levels of **Aluminium ($\text{Al}^{3+}$)**, **Manganese ($\text{Mn}^{2+}$)**, and **Iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$)**.
2. **Macronutrient Deficiencies**: Critical deficiencies of **Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$)**, **Magnesium ($\text{Mg}^{2+}$)**, and **Potassium ($\text{K}^+$)**.
3. **Phosphorus Fixation**: Available orthophosphates ($\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-$) precipitate into insoluble iron phosphate ($\text{FePO}_4$) and aluminum phosphate ($\text{AlPO}_4$).
4. **Micronutrient Anomalies**: Molybdenum ($\text{Mo}$) availability drops sharply (unlike other micronutrients whose solubility increases at low pH).

### Chemical Reclamation of Acid Soils
Amelioration requires applying agricultural liming materials to neutralize active and exchangeable $\text{H}^+$ and $\text{Al}^{3+}$ ions.

#### Agricultural Liming Materials
* **Calcite / Limestone**: Calcium Carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$).
* **Dolomite / Dolomitic Limestone**: Calcium Magnesium Carbonate ($\text{CaMg}(\text{CO}_3)_2$).
* **Burnt Lime / Quick Lime**: Calcium Oxide ($\text{CaO}$) — fast acting.
* **Hydrated Lime / Slaked Lime**: Calcium Hydroxide ($\text{Ca}(\text{OH})_2$) — fast acting.

> 💡 **Exam Pointer:** Fine-textured clay acid soils require substantially higher quantities of liming agents than coarse sandy acid soils at the same pH due to higher buffering capacity and residual acidity. **Lime Factor** ranges from **1.5 to 2.0**. Reclamation cost under RKVY is benchmarked at **₹15,000/ha** **[IBPS-AFO 2019]**.

#### Agronomic Practices & Fertilizer Management
* **Alkaline / Basic Fertilizers**: Use **Sodium Nitrate ($\text{NaNO}_3$)**, **Calcium Nitrate ($\text{Ca(NO}_3)_2$)**, or **Rock Phosphate** ($\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{F}$) **[IBPS-AFO 2022]**.
* **Acidic Fertilizer Avoidance**: Avoid continuous application of Ammonium Sulfate ($(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$) or Urea.

## 8. Total Soluble Salts (TSS) & Soil EC Diagnostic Benchmarks

Total Soluble Salts (TSS) are assessed via Electrical Conductivity ($EC$) at $25^\circ\text{C}$.

### Conversion Formula
$$\mathbf{\text{TSS (mmol/L)}} = \mathbf{EC\text{ (dS/m)} \times 10}$$

### Soil Salinity Classifications by EC ($dS/m$)

| Soil Saturation Extract $EC$ ($\text{dS/m}$) | Effect on Crop Growth & Yield Potential |
| :---: | :--- |
| **0 – 2** | Normal soil; salinity effects mostly negligible. |
| **2 – 4** | Germination and yield of very sensitive crops restricted. |
| **4 – 8** | Yields of many sensitive crops restricted **[IBPS-AFO 2018]**. |
| **8 – 16** | Only tolerant crops yield satisfactorily. |
| **$> 16$** | Extremely high TSS; only a few highly tolerant crops yield **[RRB SO 2018]**. |

### Total Soluble Solids (TSS) Direct Salinity Ratings ($\text{milli mhos/cm}^2$)

| TSS Rating ($\text{milli mhos/cm}^2$) | Diagnostic Interpretation |
| :---: | :--- |
| **$< 1.0$** | **Normal**: Non-injurious to crop growth. |
| **1.0 – 2.0** | **Critical for Germination**: May impede seedling emergence. |
| **2.0 – 4.0** | **Critical for Growth**: Injurious to sensitive crops. |
| **$> 4.0$** | **Injurious**: Harmful to almost all standard agricultural crops. |
