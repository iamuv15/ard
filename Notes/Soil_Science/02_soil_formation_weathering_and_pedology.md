## 1. Foundations of Pedology & Soil Genesis

Soil formation (Pedogenesis) is the two-stage transformation of consolidated bedrock into a dynamic, porous, natural biological medium capable of supporting plant life.

```
       [STAGE 1: WEATHERING]                      [STAGE 2: PEDOGENESIS]
Rock (R) ─────────────────> Regolith (Unconsolidated) ──────────────────> Solum (True Soil)
         Disintegration &                            Action of Active &
           Decomposition                              Passive Soil Factors
```

### Core Definitions & System Boundaries
* **Soil Genesis**: The multi-step process comprising:
  1. **Weathering**: Physical disintegration and chemical decomposition of primary rock ($R$) into unconsolidated parent material called **Regolith**.
  2. **Pedogenesis**: The development of distinct horizons and soil structures from regolith driven by active and passive soil-forming factors.
* **Solum ("True Soil")**: The upper, most weathered part of the soil profile containing active biological processes ($A + B$ horizons or $A + E + B$ horizons).
* **Regolith**: The entire mantle of unconsolidated loose rock material above solid bedrock ($A + B + C$ horizons).
* **Bedrock ($R$)**: Consolidated rock underlying the regolith.
* **Soil Fertility**: The inherent capacity of a soil to supply essential plant nutrients in adequate amounts and suitable proportions for plant growth **[UPCATET PG]**, **[BHU]**, **[AU PG]**, **[IFFCO AGT]**.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------+
| SOLUM (True Soil: A + E + B)                          |
|   Biological Activity & Pedogenic Horizons            |
+-------------------------------------------------------+  <-- REGOLITH (A + B + C)
| C HORIZON (Unconsolidated Parent Material)           |
+-------------------------------------------------------+
| R LAYER (Consolidated Bedrock)                        |
+-------------------------------------------------------+
```

> 💡 **Exam Pointer:** 
> * **Solum** = $A + B$ or $A + E + B$ (Excludes $C$ horizon)
> * **Regolith** = $A + B + C$ (Excludes $R$ layer)
> * **Master Horizons** = **5** ($O, A, E, B, C$)

## 2. Rocks, Minerals & Parent Material Dynamics

* **Primary Crustal Mineral Abundance**: **Feldspars (48%) > Quartz (36%) > Micas (10%)**.
* **Fundamental Structural Unit**: The **Silica Tetrahedron** ($\text{SiO}_4^{4-}$) serves as the basic structural block for all silicate minerals.

```
Silica Tetrahedron [SiO4]4- ---> Chains / Sheets / Frameworks ---> Primary Minerals
```

### Weathering Resistance Series (Goldich’s Dissolution Series)
Minerals exhibit differential stability against physical and chemical weathering based on their ionic arrangement and silica frame bonding.

```
[MOST RESISTANT]                                                       [LEAST RESISTANT]
Quartz ──> Muscovite ──> Biotite ──> Na/Ca Feldspars ──> Olivine ──> Calcite (Non-Silicate)
```

| Resistance Level | Mineral Name | Chemical Formula / Class | Weathering Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Most Resistant** | **Quartz** | $\text{SiO}_2$ (Framework Silicate) | Dominates the coarse sand fraction; highly non-cleavable. |
| **High Resistance** | **Muscovite** (White Mica) | $\text{KAl}_2(\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10})(\text{OH})_2$ | Highly resistant dioctahedral sheet silicate. |
| **Moderate Resistance**| **Biotite** (Black Mica) | $\text{K(Mg,Fe)}_3(\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10})(\text{OH})_2$ | Trioctahedral structure; less stable than Muscovite. |
| **Low Resistance** | **Plagioclase Feldspars** | $\text{NaAlSi}_3\text{O}_8 - \text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8$ | Readily undergoes hydrolysis and carbonation. |
| **Least Resistant (Silicate)**| **Olivine** | $(\text{Mg,Fe})_2\text{SiO}_4$ (Nesosilicate) | Rapidly breaks down under chemical weathering. |
| **Least Resistant (Non-Silicate)**| **Calcite** | $\text{CaCO}_3$ (Carbonate) | Dissolves easily in carbonated water. |

> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** **Muscovite (White Mica)** is significantly **more resistant** to weathering than **Biotite (Black Mica)** due to structural differences (dioctahedral vs. trioctahedral layers containing $Fe^{2+}/Mg^{2+}$).

## 3. Factors of Soil Formation

Dokuchaev (1883) established the first functional formula for soil formation, which was later refined by Hans Jenny (1941):

$$S = f(C, O, R, P, T, \dots)$$

Where:
* $S$ = Soil properties
* $C$ = Climate (Active)
* $O$ = Organisms / Vegetation (Active)
* $R$ = Relief / Topography (Passive)
* $P$ = Parent Material (Passive)
* $T$ = Time (Passive)

```
                       ┌── ACTIVE FACTORS ───► Climate (C), Organisms (O)
                       │   (Supply energy and moisture to drive processes)
SOIL FORMING FACTORS ──┤
                       │
                       └── PASSIVE FACTORS ──► Topography (R), Parent Material (P), Time (T)
                           (Provide matrix and boundary conditions)
```

### Relief / Topography Slope Classification
Topography influences soil development by modifying runoff, drainage, effective precipitation, and soil erosion.

| Topography / Slope Designation | Slope Angle (%) | Elevation / Landscape Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Flat to Almost Flat** | $0 - 2\%$ | Depressional / Level floodplains |
| **Gently Undulating** | $2 - 5\%$ | Mild slopes, low runoff risk |
| **Undulating** | $5 - 10\%$ | Moderate slope, controlled runoff |
| **Rolling** | $10 - 15\%$ | Rolling terrain, accelerated surface drainage |
| **Hilly / Steeply Dissected** | $15 - 30\%$ | Moderate elevation range ($< 300\text{ m}$) |
| **Mountainous** | $> 30\%$ | High elevation range ($> 300\text{ m}$), severe erosion risk |

## 4. Fundamental & Specific Pedogenic Processes

Pedogenic processes represent the chemical and physical operations that transform parent material into distinct soil profile horizons **[AAO 2018]**.

```
[BASIC DYNAMICS]
- Humification (Organic accumulation)
- Eluviation  (Outward washing from A/E) [RRB SO 2019]
- Illuviation (Inward deposition into B) [IBPS-AFO 2020]
- Horizonation (Profile differentiation)
```

### Specific Pedogenic Processes

```
                   ┌── Podzolization: Low Temp + Humid ──► Accumulates Silica in A; Leaches Fe/Al to B
                   ├── Laterization: High Temp + Rainfall ──► Leaches Silica; Accumulates Fe/Al in Solum
PEDOGENIC PATHWAYS ┼── Gleization: Poor Drainage / Waterlogged ──► Reduces Fe/Mn (Gley horizon)
                   ├── Calcification / Decalcification ──► CaCO3 Accumulation / Removal
                   └── Salinization / Desalinization ──► Soluble Salt Accumulation / Leaching
```

#### 1. Humification
The biological and chemical transformation of raw plant/animal residue into dark, amorphous **humus**.

#### 2. Eluviation ("Washing Out")
The mobilization and removal of soil constituents (clay, $Fe$, $Al$, organic matter) in solution or suspension from the upper horizons ($O$, $A$, $E$) to lower horizons by percolating water **[RRB SO 2019]**.

#### 3. Illuviation ("Washing In")
The deposition and accumulation of eluviated materials (clay, sesquioxides, organic matter) into a lower horizon (primarily the **$B$ Horizon**) **[IBPS-AFO 2020]**.

#### 4. Horizonation
The differentiation of a soil column into distinct horizontal layers (horizons) along its vertical profile.

#### 5. Calcification & Decalcification
* **Calcification**: Accumulation and precipitation of calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) in specific profile zones, common in arid/semi-arid regions.
* **Decalcification**: Removal of $\text{CaCO}_3$ or free calcium ions ($Ca^{2+}$) from the soil profile by leaching under high rainfall.

#### 6. Podzolization
* **Climatic Regime**: **Cold and humid** climates under coniferous or acidic vegetation.
* **Mechanism**: Intense leaching removes basic cations and sesquioxides ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$), leaving residual **Silica ($\text{SiO}_2$)** in the upper horizon ($E$).
* **Result**: Sesquioxides and organic matter leach downward and accumulate in the **$B$ Horizon**.

#### 7. Laterization
* **Climatic Regime**: **Hot and humid** tropical/subtropical regions with alternating wet and dry seasons.
* **Mechanism**: Intensive weathering dissolves and **removes Silica ($\text{SiO}_2$)** from upper horizons, leaving **Sesquioxides ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$)** to accumulate in the solum.
* **Result**: Highly weathered, iron-rich, low-CEC soils (Laterites).

#### 8. Gleization
* **Mechanism**: Occurs under prolonged **waterlogging** and poor drainage conditions (anaerobic environment/lack of oxygen).
* **Result**: Microbial reduction of ferric iron ($\text{Fe}^{3+}$) to ferrous iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$) produces a characteristic bluish-grey, sticky horizon known as a **Glei (Gley)** horizon.

#### 9. Salinization & Desalinization
* **Salinization**: Accumulation of soluble salts (sulfates, chlorides of $Ca, Mg, Na, K$) in upper soil horizons, forming a salic horizon in arid/semi-arid regions.
* **Desalinization**: Removal of soluble salts from soil horizons by leaching with fresh water under improved drainage conditions.

#### 10. Alkalization (Solonization) & Dealkalization (Solodization)
* **Alkalization (Solonization)**: Accumulation of sodium ions ($\text{Na}^+$) on the cation exchange complex of clay particles, leading to sodic soil conditions (high pH, poor structure).
* **Dealkalization (Solodization)**: Removal of exchangeable $\text{Na}^+$ from exchange sites via intensive leaching, causing severe **clay dispersion** and structural degradation.

#### 11. Pedoturbation
The mechanical mixing of soil materials across horizons by natural forces (e.g., biological activity, freeze-thaw cycles, or shrink-swell clay movement in Vertisols).

## 5. Master Horizons & Soil Profile Architecture

A vertical cross-section of the soil displaying all its constituent horizons from the surface down to the parent material is termed a **Soil Profile**.

```
[O Horizon] ── Organic Layer (Virgin forest soils; Absent in Arable soils) [BHU]
     │
[A Horizon] ── Topsoil / Surface Mineral Horizon (Organic accumulation + Leaching)
     │
[E Horizon] ── Zone of Maximum Eluviation (Wash-out of clay/Fe/Al; Absent in Black Soils)
     │
[B Horizon] ── Subsoil / Zone of Maximum Illuviation (Wash-in of clay/Fe/Al) [IBPS-AFO 2020]
     │
[C Horizon] ── Unconsolidated Parent Material (Regolith base)
     │
[R Layer]   ── Consolidated Bedrock
```

### Comprehensive Master Horizon Breakdown

```
       MASTER HORIZONS (5)
 ┌───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬───────┐
 O       A       E       B       C
```

#### 1. $O$ Horizon (Organic Horizon)
* **Characteristics**: Surface layer formed above the mineral soil, dominated by fresh, partially decomposed, or fully decomposed organic material.
* **Soil Organic Matter ($\text{SOM}$) Criteria**:
  * If the mineral fraction has **$> 50\%$ clay**, $\text{SOM}$ must be **$> 30\%$**.
  * If the mineral fraction has **$< 50\%$ clay**, $\text{SOM}$ must be **$> 20\%$**.
* **Occurrence**: Common in undisturbed **virgin forest soils**; **completely absent in cultivated arable soils** due to tillage operations **[BHU Exam]**.

#### 2. $A$ Horizon (Surface Mineral Horizon)
* **Characteristics**: Topmost mineral horizon adjacent to the surface.
* **Features**: Accumulation of humified organic matter intimately mixed with the mineral fraction. It exhibits maximum biological activity and initial losses of soluble salts and fine clay particles.

#### 3. $E$ Horizon (or $A_2$ Horizon - Zone of Eluviation)
* **Characteristics**: Horizon of maximum **eluviation** ("wash-out") of clay particles, iron oxides, aluminum oxides, and organic matter **[RRB SO 2019]**.
* **Features**: Bleached, light-colored horizon dominated by resistant silica and quartz sand/silt grains.
* **Distribution**: **Absent in Black Cotton Soils (Vertisols)**.

#### 4. $B$ Horizon (Subsoil - Zone of Illuviation)
* **Characteristics**: Horizon of maximum **illuviation** ("wash-in") **[IBPS-AFO 2020]**.
* **Features**: Zone of structural development and concentration of accumulated silicate clay, iron, aluminum, carbonate, gypsum, or humic complexes leached from upper layers ($A/E$).

#### 5. $C$ Horizon (Substratum)
* **Characteristics**: Unconsolidated parent material below the solum ($A + B$).
* **Features**: Relatively unaffected by pedogenic processes; outside the zone of major biological activity. May contain secondary accumulations of carbonates, sulfates, or calcium/magnesium salts.

#### 6. $R$ Layer (Bedrock)
* **Characteristics**: Underlying consolidated bedrock (Granite, Basalt, Sandstone, etc.) that may or may not match the parent material from which the solum formed.

### Master Horizon Comparative Summary

| Master Horizon | Alternative Name | Dominant Pedogenic Process | Structural & Physical Characteristics | Occurrence & Exam Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$O$ Horizon** | Organic Layer | Organic Matter Accumulation | Fresh/decomposed litter; $>20-30\%\text{ SOM}$ threshold | Present in virgin forests; **Absent in arable soils** **[BHU Exam]** |
| **$A$ Horizon** | Surface Soil | Humification & Initial Leaching | Dark surface horizon rich in humified organic material | Primary root zone in arable cropping systems |

| **$R$ Layer** | Bedrock | None (Parent Rock) | Hard, consolidated mineral rock layer | Underlying foundation |

## 6. Associated Soil Chemistry & Physical Benchmarks

### Essential Soil Physical Constants & Conversion Benchmarks
* **Solar Constant**: Rate of solar energy reaching the atmospheric threshold = **$2\text{ cal/cm}^2/\text{min}$**.
* **Surface Tension of Pure Water**: **$72.7\text{ dyne/cm}^2$** at $25^\circ\text{C}$.
* **Water Molecule Structure**: Hexagonal lattice geometry in solid state with a bond angle of **$104.5^\circ$**.
* **Colloidal Size Limit**: Particle diameter threshold = **$< 1\ \mu\text{m}$ ($< 0.001\text{ mm}$)**.
* **Soil vs. Air Temperature**: Mean annual soil temperature is approximately **$1^\circ\text{C}$ higher** than mean annual air temperature.
* **Standard Soil Temp Measurement Depths**: International Meteorological Organization specifies **$10\text{ cm}, 20\text{ cm}, 50\text{ cm},$ and $100\text{ cm}$**.
* **Soil Redox Potential ($E_h$) Thresholds**:
  * Well-aerated / Well-drained soils: **$+400\text{ to }+700\text{ mV}$**
  * Submerged / Waterlogged soils: **$-250\text{ to }-300\text{ mV}$**
* **Permissible Soil Loss Limit**: **$12\text{ t/ha/year}$** in India.
* **Volumetric Conversion Benchmark**:
  $$1\text{ ha-cm} = 100,000\text{ Liters} = 100\text{ m}^3$$

---

### Organic Matter Conversions & Carbon-Nitrogen Ratios
* **Van Bemmelen Factor**: Organic Matter ($\text{OM}$) content is derived from Organic Carbon ($\text{OC}$) using the formula:
  $$\text{Organic Matter (\%)} = \text{Organic Carbon (\%)} \times 1.724 \quad \mathbf{[IBPS-AFO\ 2016]}$$
* **Soil Organic Matter Content**: Typically **$1\%\text{ to }5\%$** in Indian topsoils **[BHU 2014]**.
* **Humus Chemical Composition**: Lignoprotein complex consisting of **$40-45\%$ Lignin** and **$30-33\%$ Protein**. Dark color is primarily due to **Humin**.
* **C:N Ratio Benchmarks**:
  * **Tropical Soils**: **10:1**
  * **Microbial Biomass**: Lowest C:N ratio among organic sources **[RRB SO 2020]**
  * **Immobilization Threshold**: **$> 30:1$** (causes net nitrogen immobilization)

## Previous Year Questions (PYQs) & Solved Rapid-Fire MCQs

### Question 1 [IBPS-AFO 2020]
**Which master horizon represents the primary zone of illuviation where eluviated materials like silicate clays, iron, aluminum, and organic matter accumulate?**
A. $O$ Horizon  
B. $A$ Horizon  
C. $E$ Horizon  
D. $B$ Horizon  
E. $C$ Horizon  

**Answer:** D  
**Pedagogical Rationale:** The $B$ horizon is officially defined in pedology as the subsoil layer of maximum illuviation ("washing in"), where materials transported downward from the $A$ or $E$ horizons precipitate and accumulate.

---

### Question 2 [RRB SO 2019]
**What is the term used when soil constituents in solution or suspension wash out or percolate from upper horizons to lower horizons?**
A. Illuviation  
B. Eluviation  
C. Pedoturbation  
D. Gleization  
E. Calcification  

**Answer:** B  
**Pedagogical Rationale:** Eluviation (derived from Latin *ex*, out, and *lavere*, to wash) is the exit/removal of soil components in suspension or solution from an upper horizon (such as $A$ or $E$). Illuviation refers to the entry/deposition into a lower horizon ($B$).

---

### Question 3 [RRB SO 2020]
**Parent material deposited at the base of steep slopes and hills under the direct influence of gravity is classified as:**
A. Alluvial Soil  
B. Aeolian Soil  
C. Colluvial Soil  
D. Glacial Soil  
E. Lacustrine Soil  

**Answer:** C  
**Pedagogical Rationale:** Colluvium (or colluvial soil) consists of coarse, unsorted rock fragments and soil material transported down steep hillsides under gravity. Alluvial is transported by flowing water, Aeolian by wind, and Lacustrine by lake sedimentation.
