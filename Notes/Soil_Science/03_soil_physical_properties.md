## 1. Soil Texture & Particle Size Distribution

Soil texture refers to the relative proportions (percentage by weight) of the three principal soil separates: **Sand**, **Silt**, and **Clay**. It is a fundamental, permanent physical property of soil that **cannot be altered** by standard agricultural practices under field conditions.

```
                  SOIL SEPARATES CLASSIFICATION
                                │
       ┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────┐
       ▼                                                 ▼
USDA Classification                               ISSS / European System
(Silt: 0.002 - 0.05 mm)                           (Silt: 0.002 - 0.02 mm)
```

### Major Soil Textural Groups & Classes

Soil textural classes are divided into broad categories based on the dominance of specific separates:

#### 1. Sands
- Sand separates constitute at least **$70\%$** by weight, and clay separates constitute **$\le 15\%$** **[IBPS-AFO 2019]**.
- Includes two official classes: **Sandy** and **Loamy Sand** (with subclasses: *Loamy Fine Sand* and *Loamy Very Fine Sand*).

#### 2. Silts
- Contains at least **$80\%$ Silt** and **$\le 12\%$ Clay**.
- Contains only one recognized textural class: **Silt**.

#### 3. Clays
- Must contain at least **$35\%$ Clay** (in most cases $\ge 40\%$).
- Dominant characteristics are governed by the clay fraction.
- Textural classes: **Clay**, **Sandy Clay**, and **Silty Clay**.

#### 4. Loams
- Defined as an optimal mixture of sand, silt, and clay particles exhibiting physical properties of all three separates in roughly equal proportions.

> ⚠️ **Common Trap:** 
> A **Loam** soil **does NOT contain equal percentages** of sand, silt, and clay by weight. Instead, it **exhibits equal physical properties** of sand, silt, and clay because clay has a far higher specific surface area and chemical reactivity per unit mass **[MCAER PGCET]**.

## 2. Soil Structure & Structural Units

Soil structure describes the arrangement and organization of primary soil particles (sand, silt, clay) into secondary units or aggregates called **peds**. Unlike texture, soil structure is dynamic and **highly sensitive to management practices** (tillage, cropping systems, irrigation, liming, and organic additions).

```
                 SOIL STRUCTURAL TERMINOLOGY
                              │
       ┌──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┐
       ▼                                             ▼
     PEDS                                          CLODS
(Naturally formed aggregates)             (Artificially formed via
                                            mechanical tillage)
```

### Classification of Soil Structural Types

```
                        STRUCTURAL TYPES & LOCATIONS
                                     │
         ┌───────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────┐
         ▼                           ▼                           ▼
   Spheroidal                  Platy / Plate-like            Blocky / Prismatic
 (A Horizon Topsoil)          (Lower A / Forest Soils)          (B Horizon)
```

1. **Granular & Crumb**:
   - **Shape**: Spheres or rounded polyhedrons. Crumb is more porous than granular.
   - **Horizon / Occurrence**: Predominantly found in the surface **A-horizon** of fertile agricultural soils.
2. **Platy**:
   - **Shape**: Thin, horizontal plates or sheets stacked vertically.
   - **Horizon / Occurrence**: Commonly found in virgin **forest soils**, sub-surface **A-horizon**, and **claypan** layers.
3. **Prismatic & Columnar**:
   - **Shape**: Vertical pillars or columns. Prismatic forms have flat tops, whereas Columnar forms have rounded tops.
   - **Horizon / Occurrence**: Found in the **B-horizon** of arid and semi-arid soils containing high exchangeable **Sodium ($\text{Na}^+$)**.
4. **Blocky & Subangular Blocky**:
   - **Shape**: Cube-like structural units with sharp (angular) or rounded (subangular) edges.
   - **Horizon / Occurrence**: Predominantly present in the **B-horizon**.
5. **Wedge**:
   - **Shape**: Ellipsoidal structural units overlapping at low angles.
   - **Horizon / Occurrence**: Characteristic of soils high in expanding 2:1 clay minerals, such as **Montmorillonite** (Vertisols/Black soils).
6. **Single Grain**:
   - **Shape**: Structureless condition where particles remain unaggregated.
   - **Horizon / Occurrence**: Beach sands and active wind-blown sand dunes.

## 3. Soil Consistence, Plasticity & Cohesion

### Soil Consistence
Soil consistence refers to the resistance of a soil to mechanical stress or manipulation at varying moisture contents **[IBPS-AFO 2015]**. It reflects the combined action of internal **cohesive** and **adhesive** molecular forces.
- **Cohesion**: Attraction between like molecules (soil particle to soil particle).
- **Adhesion**: Attraction between unlike molecules (soil particle to water molecule).
- Consistence is categorized across three standard moisture levels: **Wet**, **Moist**, and **Dry**.

### Soil Plasticity
- **Definition**: The capacity of moist soil to undergo deformation under applied force without cracking or rupturing, and to retain the newly molded shape after the force is released.
- **Threshold Condition**: A soil must contain a minimum of **$15\%$ Clay** to exhibit plastic properties.

## 4. Soil Density: Particle Density vs. Bulk Density

Soil density parameters govern porosity, root penetration resistance, and soil compaction dynamics.

```
                             SOIL VOLUMETRIC FRACTIONS
    ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │              SOLID FRACTION             │                PORE SPACE               │
    │         (Mineral + Organic)             │          (Air-Filled + Water-Filled)     │
    └─────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────┘
    ◄────────────────── PARTICLE DENSITY ($\rho_p$) ──────────────────►
    (Solids Volume Only)
    ◄───────────────────────────────── BULK DENSITY ($\rho_b$) ─────────────────────────►
    (Total Soil Volume: Solids + Pores)
```

### 1. Particle Density ($\rho_p$ / True Density)
- Definition: Mass per unit volume of the **solid phase** of soil (excluding pore space).
- Standard Mean Benchmark: **$2.65\text{ g/cm}^3$** (or $\text{g/cc}$) for predominant mineral soils dominated by quartz and feldspars **[UPPCS]**.
- **Particle Density of Organic Soils**: Ranges from **$2.1\text{ to }2.4\text{ g/cm}^3$**.
- **Particle Density of Pure Soil Organic Matter (SOM)**: Ranges from **$1.1\text{ to }1.4\text{ g/cm}^3$**.

### 2. Bulk Density ($\rho_b$)
- Definition: Oven-dry mass of soil per unit **total volume** (inclusive of both solids and pore spaces).
- Relationship: Bulk density is **always lower** than particle density ($\text{BD} < \text{PD}$) due to the inclusion of pore volume.
- Key Reference Values:
  - Bulk Density of Organic Matter: **$0.5\text{ g/cm}^3$**
  - Problematic / Highly Organic Soils: **$< 1.0\text{ g/cm}^3$**
  - Compacted / Dense Soils: **$> 2.0\text{ g/cm}^3$**
  - Severely Compacted Subsoils: **$2.0\text{ g/cm}^3$**

> 💡 **Exam Pointer:** 
> Bulk density varies inversely with particle fineness. As soil texture transitions from coarse (sand) to fine (clay), bulk density **decreases** because clay soils possess higher total micro-porosity and better structural aggregation.

### Textural Classes & Representative Bulk Density Values

| Textural Class | Typical Bulk Density ($\text{g/cm}^3$) | Exam Tags & Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sandy Soil** | **$1.60$** | High macro-porosity, but low total porosity **[Raj Pre-PG 2014]** |
| **Loam** | **$1.40$** ($1.447$) | Optimal balance of macro and micro pores |
| **Silt Loam** | **$1.30$** ($1.350$) | Intermediate density range |
| **Clay** | **$1.10$** ($1.258$) | High total porosity, low bulk density |

### Fundamental Volumetric Mathematical Equations

1. **Percentage Solid Space**:
   $$\text{Solid Space (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Bulk Density}}{\text{Particle Density}} \right) \times 100 = \left( \frac{\rho_b}{\rho_p} \right) \times 100$$

2. **Percentage Total Pore Space (Porosity, $E$)**:
   $$\text{Pore Space (\%)} = 100 - \text{Solid Space (\%)} = \left[ 1 - \left( \frac{\text{Bulk Density}}{\text{Particle Density}} \right) \right] \times 100$$

Soil porosity represents the volume percentage of total soil bulk volume that is unoccupied by solid mineral or organic matter **[ICAR PhD]**.
