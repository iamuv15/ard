### Keyword 1: Igneous Rocks

**Platform Definition:**
> "Igneous rocks are primary rocks formed by the cooling, solidification, and crystallization of molten magma beneath the Earth's surface or lava on the surface."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix 

| Rock Subtype | Cooling Location | Silica Content (%) | Representative Examples | 
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| **Acid / Felsic** | Intrusive / Extrusive | $> 65\%$ | Granite, Rhyolite | 
| **Intermediate** | Intrusive / Extrusive | $52\% - 65\%$ | Syenite, Diorite, Andesite | 
| **Basic / Mafic** | Intrusive / Extrusive | $45\% - 52\%$ | Gabbro, Basalt | 
| **Ultrabasic** | Intrusive | $< 45\%$ | Peridotite, Dunite | 

### Key Characteristics & Rules 
- **Primary Source**: Constitute about 95% of the Earth's upper crust by volume. 
- **Weathering Susceptibility**: Basic rocks weather much faster than acid rocks due to higher iron, magnesium, and calcium content.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Classification by Silica**: Acid (>65% SiO2; e.g. Granite, Rhyolite), Intermediate (56-65% SiO2; e.g. Syenite, Diorite), Basic (40-55% SiO2; e.g. Basalt, Gabbro), Ultrabasic (<40% SiO2; e.g. Peridotite).
- **Intrusive (Plutonic)**: Slow cooling beneath the crust allows large visible crystals (coarse texture: Granite, Gabbro).
- **Extrusive (Volcanic)**: Rapid cooling on the Earth surface creates microscopic crystals (fine texture: Basalt, Obsidian).

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** Granite is frequently misidentified as a sedimentary or metamorphic rock; it is an **intrusive igneous acid rock**.

### Keyword 2: Sedimentary Rocks

**Platform Definition:**
> "Secondary rocks formed through the weathering, transportation, deposition, and subsequent lithification (cementation and compaction) of pre-existing primary or secondary rocks."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix

| Rock Type / Example | Primary Mineral Composition | Mode of Formation | Classic Economic or Agricultural Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sandstone** | Quartz ($	ext{SiO}_2$), Feldspar traces | Cementation of sand-sized mineral grains | Excellent aquifer medium; parent material for coarse sandy soils |
| **Shale** | Clay minerals, Quartz, Micas | Compaction and lithification of silt and clay | Most abundant sedimentary rock; parent material for fine-textured clay soils |
| **Limestone** | Calcite ($	ext{CaCO}_3$), Dolomite | Precipitation or accumulation of organic skeletal remains | Source of agricultural lime; neutralizes soil acidity |
| **Conglomerate** | Rounded gravels, pebbles, quartz cement | Lithification of coarse, water-worn gravel fragments | High permeability, non-agricultural stony lands |

### Key Characteristics & Rules
- **Lithification Process**: Entails compaction under overlying burden pressure and cementation via cementing agents like silica, iron oxides, and calcium carbonate.
- **Stratification**: The defining structural feature of sedimentary rocks is bedding or layering, representing successive depositional episodes over geological time.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Lithification Sequence**: Weathering &rarr; Erosion &rarr; Transportation &rarr; Sedimentation &rarr; Diagenesis / Cementation.
- **Key Examples**: Sandstone (cemented quartz sand), Shale (consolidated clay/silt), Limestone (CaCO3), Dolomite (CaMg(CO3)2), Conglomerate (rounded gravels).
- **Stratification**: Exhibit distinct bedding planes and frequently contain organic fossils.

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - **Granite is NOT Sedimentary**: Examiners frequently trap students by listing granite under sedimentary choices; granite is strictly an *intrusive igneous rock*.
> - **Shale vs. Slate**: Shale is sedimentary, but when subjected to intense regional metamorphism, it converts into *slate* (a metamorphic rock).

### Keyword 3: Metamorphic Rocks

**Platform Definition:**
> "Rocks formed from pre-existing igneous, sedimentary, or older metamorphic rocks through intense heat, high pressure, and chemically active fluids without complete melting."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix

| Parent Rock | Metamorphic Equivalent | Metamorphic Type / Process | Key Mineral Transformations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Granite** | **Gneiss** | Regional Metamorphism | Segregation of light (quartz/feldspar) and dark (biotite/hornblende) minerals into bands |
| **Shale / Basalt** | **Schist** | Regional Metamorphism | Development of foliation, parallel alignment of flaky minerals like micas |
| **Sandstone** | **Quartzite** | Thermal (Contact) Metamorphism | Recrystallization of quartz grains into an extremely durable, hard rock mass |
| **Limestone** | **Marble** | Thermal / Contact Metamorphism | Recrystallization of calcite crystals, eliminating original fossils |

### Key Characteristics & Rules
- **Foliation**: The parallel arrangement of mineral grains or structural bands commonly induced by directed differential pressure during regional metamorphism.
- **Hardness and Durability**: Metamorphic rocks like quartzite and gneiss exhibit high resistance to mechanical weathering.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Classic Transformations Tested in Exams**:
  * Granite (Igneous) &rarr; **Gneiss** (Foliated band structure)
  * Basalt / Shale &rarr; **Schist** (Platy mineral alignment)
  * Sandstone (Sedimentary) &rarr; **Quartzite** (Non-foliated, fused quartz grains)
  * Shale (Sedimentary) &rarr; **Slate** (Dense, planar slaty cleavage)
  * Limestone (Sedimentary) &rarr; **Marble** (Recrystallized calcite)

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - **Marble Parentage**: Examiners often ask for the parent rock of marble; it is **limestone** (sedimentary), NOT granite.
> - **Quartzite Origin**: Quartzite forms from *sandstone* via thermal metamorphism, retaining its silica-rich framework but gaining extreme mechanical hardness.

### Keyword 4: Basalt

**Platform Definition:**
> "An extrusive, fine-grained, basic igneous rock rich in iron, magnesium, and calcium, serving as the dominant parent material for Vertisols (Black Cotton Soils)."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix

| Parameter / Property | Metric / Description | Exam Relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Silica ($	ext{SiO}_2$) Content** | $40\% \text{ to } 55\%$ (Basic Rock) | Classifies basalt as a basic, low-silica igneous rock |
| **Primary Minerals** | Plagioclase feldspar, Augite (Pyroxene), Olivine | Rapidly weatherable due to high mafic mineral content |
| **Texture & Structure** | Aphanitic (fine-grained), glassy to microlitic | Rapid surface cooling prevents large crystal formation |
| **Associated Soil Order** | Vertisols (Black Cotton Soils) | High smectite clay formation, high shrink-swell capacity |

### Key Characteristics & Rules
- **Weathering Pathway**: Rapid chemical breakdown of calcic plagioclase and pyroxenes releases abundant $\text{Ca}^{2+}$, $\text{Mg}^{2+}$, and iron oxides, fostering montmorillonite clay authigenesis.
- **Color**: Dark grey to black color attributed to high concentrations of ferro-magnesian minerals.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Deccan Traps & Regur**: Extensive volcanic basalt flows in Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Gujarat formed the parent material for Indian **Black Soils (Vertisols)**.
- **Mineral Makeup**: Rich in plagioclase feldspars, augite/pyroxenes, and olivine, with low silica (40-55%) and high Fe, Mg, Ca, causing rapid weathering.

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - **Extrusive vs. Intrusive**: Basalt is an **extrusive (volcanic)** rock formed by lava cooling on the surface, whereas **Gabbro** is its chemically identical *intrusive (plutonic)* counterpart.
> - **Soil Association**: Basalt weather products give rise to smectite-rich dark soils prone to deep cracking during dry seasons (self-ploughing property).

### Keyword 5: Granite

**Platform Definition:**
> "A coarse-grained, intrusive acidic igneous rock composed mainly of quartz, alkali feldspars, and micas, forming the primary foundation of continental crust."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix

| Parameter / Property | Metric / Description | Exam Relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Silica ($	ext{SiO}_2$) Content** | $> 65\%$ (Acid Rock) | High silica content imparts high resistance to chemical weathering |
| **Primary Minerals** | Quartz ($30\%\text{--}40\%$), Orthoclase Feldspar ($50\%$), Muscovite/Biotite | Major source of primary plant nutrients ($\text{K}$) and soil sand fractions |
| **Texture & Structure** | Phaneritic (coarse-grained, visible crystals) | Slow cooling of magma deep within the Earth's crust |
| **Associated Soil Order** | Alfisols, Ultisols, Entisols | Weathering yields coarse, sandy-loam, acidic soils |

### Key Characteristics & Rules
- **Resilience**: Due to high quartz and crystalline potassium feldspar content, granite weathers mechanically into sandy residues and chemically at a very slow rate.
- **Intrusive Nature**: Solidifies well beneath the Earth's surface under high confining pressures.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Red Soil Genesis**: Granite and granite-gneiss are the primary parent rocks of Indian **Red Soils (Alfisols)** in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh.
- **Silica & Minerals**: Acidic rock (>65% SiO2) composed primarily of quartz (clear/white), orthoclase feldspar (pink/buff), and mica (black biotite/silver muscovite).

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - **Rock Category Trap**: Always remember that granite is an **igneous rock**, never a sedimentary rock, despite common student confusion in agricultural competitive exams.
> - **Weathering Residue**: Weathering of granite does not yield sticky clay directly; it primarily produces quartz-rich sand and kaolinitic/illitic clays.

### Keyword 6: Quartz

**Platform Definition:**
> "A highly durable tectosilicate mineral composed of silicon dioxide ($\text{SiO}_2$) that exhibits extreme resistance to both physical and chemical weathering."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix

| Parameter / Property | Metric / Description | Exam Relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chemical Formula** | $\text{SiO}_2$ (Silicon dioxide) | Pure framework silicate without structural metallic cations |
| **Hardness (Mohs Scale)** | **7** | Harder than glass; resists scratching and physical abrasion |
| **Cleavage & Fracture** | Conchoidal fracture, **no cleavage** | Breaks along curved surfaces rather than flat planes |
| **Weathering Position** | **Most resistant** (Goldich's Series) | Dominates the coarse sand and silt fractions of soils worldwide |

### Key Characteristics & Rules
- **Nutrient Contribution**: Quartz contributes zero plant-available nutrients ($\text{N, P, K, Ca, Mg}$), acting purely as a physical structural skeleton in soils.
- **Persistence**: Because $\text{Si-O}$ covalent bonds are exceptionally strong, quartz accumulates persistently in residual soils.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Mohs Hardness**: Exactly **7**; lacks cleavage, exhibits conchoidal fracture.
- **Extreme Weathering Resistance**: Positioned at the very top of Goldich's dissolution stability series; dominates the coarse and fine sand fractions of soils.

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - **Nutrient Source Trap**: Do not assume quartz supplies plant-available silica directly in significant amounts; its dissolution rate is extremely slow under normal soil pH ranges.
> - **Cleavage Confusion**: Quartz has **no cleavage**, which is a key diagnostic property distinguishing it from feldspars and micas.

### Keyword 7: Feldspar

**Platform Definition:**
> "The most abundant group of framework silicates (tectosilicates) in the Earth's crust, serving as the primary natural mineral reserve of potassium, sodium, and calcium in soils."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix

| Feldspar Sub-Group | Chemical Formula / Composition | Cation Released upon Weathering | Exam Relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Orthoclase / Microcline** | $\text{KAlSi}_3\text{O}_8$ | Potassium ($\text{K}^+$) | Primary native source of soil potassium |
| **Albite (Plagioclase)** | $\text{NaAlSi}_3\text{O}_8$ | Sodium ($\text{Na}^+$) | Found in igneous and metamorphic rocks |
| **Anorthite (Plagioclase)** | $\text{CaAl}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_8$ | Calcium ($\text{Ca}^{2+}$) | Found in basic rocks like basalt |

### Key Characteristics & Rules
- **Weathering Mechanism**: Undergoes hydrolysis and carbonation, wherein $\text{K}^+$, $\text{Na}^+$, or $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ions are released into soil solution, leaving behind secondary clay minerals (kaolinite/illite).
- **Crustal Abundance**: Comprise approximately $48\%$ of the Earth's total crustal mineral weight.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Crustal Dominance**: **48% of the Earth's crust** by weight, making it the single most abundant mineral group.
- **Potassium Reserve**: **Orthoclase** ($KAlSi_3O_8$) is the primary source of natural soil potassium.
- **Plagioclase Series**: Solid solution from Albite ($NaAlSi_3O_8$) to Anorthite ($CaAl_2Si_2O_8$), supplying soil Ca and Na.

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - **Weathering Rate**: Unlike quartz, feldspars have **moderate to low resistance** to chemical weathering in Goldich's Series due to the presence of alkali/alkaline earth cations within their lattice cavities.
> - **Potassium Reserve**: Orthoclase represents the *mineral reserve* pool of potassium, distinct from exchangeable and water-soluble potassium.

### Keyword 8: Mica

**Platform Definition:**
> "A group of sheet silicate (phyllosilicate) primary minerals characterized by perfect basal cleavage and a 2:1 layer lattice structure, acting as a major reserve of soil potassium."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix 

| Mineral Sub-Type | Chemical Formula | Basal Spacing (Å) | Weathering Resistance | Potassium Content (%) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Muscovite (White Mica)** | $\text{KAl}_2(\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10})(\text{OH})_2$ | 10.0 | High | ~8 - 11% |
| **Biotite (Black Mica)** | $\text{K}(\text{Mg,Fe})_3(\text{AlSi}_3\text{O}_{10})(\text{OH})_2$ | 10.0 | Low to Moderate | ~6 - 9% |

### Key Characteristics & Rules 
- **Structural Arrangement**: 2:1 expanding-type phyllosilicate framework where potassium ions ($\text{K}^+$) are tightly locked in the interlayer hexagonal cavities, satisfying net negative charges.
- **Weathering Sequence**: Biotite weathers significantly faster than muscovite due to the presence of ferrous iron ($\text{Fe}^{2+}$), which undergoes oxidation and structural destabilization.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Phyllosilicate Architecture**: Perfect one-directional basal cleavage allowing thin, flexible sheets.
- **Muscovite vs Biotite**: Muscovite (dioctahedral white mica) is highly resistant to weathering; Biotite (trioctahedral black mica, rich in Fe2+ and Mg2+) weathers rapidly via oxidation.

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - Students often confuse muscovite and biotite weathering rates; remember **Muscovite is much more resistant** than biotite due to lack of Fe/Mg substitution in the octahedral layer.
> - Interlayer $\text{K}^+$ in micas can be replaced by hydrated $\text{H}^+$ or $\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$ during weathering, transitioning micas into **vermiculite** or **illite**.

### Keyword 9: Olivine

**Platform Definition:**
> "An iron-magnesium silicate belonging to the nesosilicate (isolated tetrahedron) group, highly susceptible to chemical weathering and serving as one of the first minerals to crystallize in Bowen's Reaction Series."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix 

| Parameter | Metric / Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Chemical Composition** | $(\text{Mg, Fe})_2\text{SiO}_4$ (Magnesium Iron Silicate) |
| **Structural Group** | Nesosilicate (Isolated $\text{[SiO}_4\text{]}^{4-}$ tetrahedra linked by divalent cations) |
| **Goldich Position** | Least resistant (extreme base of Goldich's weathering series) |
| **Cleavage** | Poor to absent (fractures conchoidally) |

### Key Characteristics & Rules 
- **Weathering Profile**: Highly vulnerable to hydrolysis and carbonation because of exposed divalent $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ and $\text{Mg}^{2+}$ ions, releasing plant-available magnesium and iron rapidly.
- **Occurrence**: Abundant in basic and ultrabasic igneous rocks such as gabbro and peridotite.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Goldich & Bowen Contrast**: Crystallizes first in Bowen's reaction series at highest temperatures, making it the **least stable / most rapidly weathered** primary silicate mineral under Earth surface conditions.
- **Nesosilicate Structure**: Individual isolated [SiO4]4- tetrahedra bonded only by Fe2+ and Mg2+ cations, easily cleaved by acidic water into serpentine.

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - Olivine is frequently tested as the **least resistant primary mineral** to chemical weathering under humid temperate conditions.
> - Do not confuse olivine's isolated tetrahedral structure with complex silicates; it lacks shared oxygen atoms between tetrahedra.

### Keyword 10: Amphiboles

**Platform Definition:**
> "A group of dark-colored, double-chain inosilicate minerals containing hydroxyl groups, commonly found in intermediate and basic igneous and metamorphic rocks."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix 

| Parameter | Metric / Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Representative Mineral** | Hornblende $\text{Ca}_2(\text{Mg,Fe},-\text{Al})_5(\text{Al,Si})_8\text{O}_{22}(\text{OH})_2$ |
| **Structural Type** | Inosilicate (Double-chain structure sharing oxygen atoms, $\text{Si}_4\text{O}_{11}$ chains) |
| **Cleavage Angle** | Characteristic prismatic cleavage intersecting at **56° and 124°** |
| **Weathering Stability** | Low to moderate (weathers faster than pyroxenes in some pedogenic contexts, but more stable than olivine) |

### Key Characteristics & Rules 
- **Nutrient Contribution**: Serves as a significant source of calcium, magnesium, and iron in young soils.
- **Weathering Behavior**: Undergoes rapid oxidation of structural $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ and hydration, forming secondary iron oxides like goethite.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Double-Chain Inosilicate**: Typical member is **Hornblende** with characteristic 56° and 124° cleavage angles.
- **Weathering Behavior**: Moderate to low resistance; significant source of iron, magnesium, and calcium in soil genesis.

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - Remember the cleavage angle distinction: Amphiboles show intersecting cleavage at **56°/124°**, whereas Pyroxenes show cleavage at nearly **90°**.

### Keyword 11: Calcite

**Platform Definition:**
> "A primary carbonate mineral composed of calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$) that dissolves readily in acidic soil solutions and buffers soil pH."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix 

| Parameter | Metric / Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Chemical Formula** | $\text{CaCO}_3$ (Trigonal crystal system) |
| **Mohs Hardness** | 3.0 (Soft; readily scratched by a copper coin) |
| **Acid Reaction** | Vigorous effervescence with cold, dilute $1\text{N } \text{HCl}$ |
| **Solubility** | High in carbonated water ($\text{H}_2\text{CO}_3$), precipitating in arid/semi-arid calcic horizons |

### Key Characteristics & Rules 
- **Pedogenic Role**: Accumulates in the subsoil of arid and semi-arid regions to form K-horizons or caliche layers.
- **Buffering Action**: Neutralizes soil acidity through the release of bicarbonate and calcium ions.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Carbonate Chemistry**: $CaCO_3$, effervesces vigorously with dilute (0.1 N) HCl.
- **Soil Accumulation**: Forms diagnostic calcic horizons (Bk) and kankar nodules in semi-arid and arid soils.

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - Calcite is a **non-silicate** primary mineral. Questions often falsely group it under primary alumino-silicates.
> - Effervescence with $\text{HCl}$ is the definitive field diagnostic test for calcite and limestone parent materials.

### Keyword 12: Primary Minerals

**Platform Definition:**
> "Minerals that formed at elevated temperatures and pressures during the crystallization of molten magma and persist in soils without fundamental structural alteration."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix 

| Mineral Category | Representative Examples | Dominant Soil Particle Size Fraction | Weathering Susceptibility |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tectosilicates** | Quartz, Orthoclase Feldspar | Coarse Sand & Silt | Very Low to Moderate |
| **Phyllosilicates** | Muscovite, Biotite | Silt & Fine Sand | Moderate to High |
| **Inosilicates** | Hornblende, Augite | Fine Sand & Silt | High |
| **Nesosilicates** | Olivine | Fine Sand | Extremely High |

### Key Characteristics & Rules 
- **Abundance**: Dominate the sand and silt fractions of mineral soils.
- **Nutrient Reserves**: Act as long-term, slow-release reservoirs for essential macro- and micronutrients.

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Particle Size Locus**: Concentrated primarily in the **sand (0.02 - 2.0 mm)** and **silt (0.002 - 0.02 mm)** fractions of soil.
- **Essential vs Accessory**: Essential minerals (Feldspar, Quartz, Mica) define the rock; Accessory minerals (Tourmaline, Zircon, Magnetite) occur in trace amounts (<1-2%).

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - **Quartz** is a primary mineral, but clay minerals derived from weathering are **secondary minerals**.
> - Primary minerals are absent or severely depleted in highly weathered ultisols and oxisols.

---

### Keyword 13: Secondary Minerals

**Platform Definition:**
> "Minerals synthesized through the weathering of pre-existing primary minerals or precipitated from solution within the soil profile."

**High-Yield Study Notes & Comparative Matrices:**
### High-Yield Exam Matrix 

| Mineral Class | Common Examples | Structural Type / Chemical Formula | Dominant Soil Fraction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Silicate Clays** | Kaolinite, Montmorillonite, Illite | 1:1 and 2:1 Phyllosilicates | Clay ($< 0.002\text{ mm}$) |
| **Iron Oxides** | Hematite, Goethite | $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{FeOOH}$ | Clay and Silt |
| **Aluminum Oxides**| Gibbsite | $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ | Clay |
| **Evaporites** | Gypsum, Calcite | $\text{CaSO}_4\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{CaCO}_3$ | Salt-affected soils |

### Key Characteristics & Rules 
- **Colloidal Properties**: Possess high specific surface areas and net negative charges, driving cation exchange capacity (CEC).
- **Pedogenic Indicators**: Reflect the intensity of weathering and drainage conditions (e.g., gibbsite indicates extreme leaching/ferralitization).

**Authoritative Exam Pointers:**
- **Colloidal Dominance**: Concentrated in the **clay fraction (<0.002 mm / <2 µm)**.
- **Major Classes**: Layer silicate clays (Kaolinite, Montmorillonite, Illite), hydrous sesquioxides (Hematite Fe2O3, Goethite FeOOH, Gibbsite Al(OH)3), and carbonates/sulfates (Calcite, Gypsum).

**Examiner Traps & PYQ Alerts:**
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap & High-Yield Rules:** 
> - **Gibbsite** and **Goethite** are secondary minerals characteristic of tropical latosols/oxisols.
> - Secondary minerals dictate the physical plasticity, cohesion, and shrink-swell capacity of agricultural soils.
