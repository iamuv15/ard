## 3. Comprehensive Disease Matrix

| Disease Name | Causal Organism / Agent | How Contracted / Transmission | Incubation Period | First & Key Signs | Affected Animals | Preventive & Treatment Measures |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rinderpest** | Virus | Direct contact or contaminated material with lesion discharge | 3–15 days | High fever, blood-stained diarrhea, discharge from lesions | Cattle, buffaloes, sometimes sheep and goats | Vaccination |
| **Foot & Mouth Disease (FMD)** | Aphthovirus (Virus) | Direct contact or contaminated material with lesion discharge | 3–8 days | Salivation, sores on feet and tongue, lameness | All domestic animals (sheep and goats less susceptible) | Segregation, sanitary measures, vaccination |
| **Anthrax** | *Bacillus anthracis* (Bacteria) | Water/food contaminated with blood/excretions or wound infection | 1–3 days or longer | Sudden death or very high fever | Cattle, buffaloes, sheep, goats, horses | Annual vaccination. Use 3% peracetic acid, 10% caustic soda, or 10% formalin. *Never open carcass.* |
| **Black Quarter (BQ)** | *Clostridium chauvoei* (Bacteria) | Water/food contaminated with blood/excretions or wound infection | Few days | Swelling in hind/fore quarter, lameness, muscles trembling with violent twitching, gas gangrene, death before 48 hours | Cattle and buffaloes, sheep to some extent | Biannual vaccination, Penicillin |
| **Trypanosomiasis (Surra, Nagana, etc.)** | Protozoa | Infective flies | 2–5 weeks | Intermittent fever, un-thriftiness | All domestic animals | Chemicals, eradication of flies |
| **Contagious Abortion (Brucellosis / Bang's Disease)** | *Brucella abortus* (Bacteria) | Food, water contaminated by discharges and aborted fetus | 21 days | Abortion at 5 months, full-time stillbirths, retained afterbirths | Cattle and buffaloes | Vaccination before breeding. Antibiotics (Streptomycin, Aureomycin). Attenuated live vaccine in female calves (4–12 months). |
| **Bovine Piroplasmosis (Redwater, Texas fever, etc.)** | Protozoa | Discharged and aborted fetus contamination, ticks | 1–4 weeks | Fever, red and black urine | Cattle, sheep, goats | Vaccination before breeding |
| **Pleuro-pneumonia of Goats** | Bacterial / Inhalation / Contact | Direct contact or nasal discharge | 2–6 days | Fever, nasal discharge | Goats | Sanitary segregation |
| **Sheep Pox** | Capripoxvirus (Poxviridae) | Inhalation / broken skin via contaminated material | 2–7 days | High fever, discharge from eyes and nose, pox lesions, papules, rhinitis, conjunctivitis | Sheep | Vaccination |
| **Swine Pox** | Virus | Ingesting or inhaling contaminated material | 6–9 days | High fever, blotched skin, diarrhea | Pigs | Sanitary segregation and destruction of affected animal |
| **Fowl Plague** | Virus | Direct contact via eating/inhaling contaminated material | 6–9 days | Sudden death, discharge from eyes/nose, inability to stand | Pigs, ducks, geese | Sanitary segregation |
| **Fowl Cholera** | Bacteria | Direct contact via eating/inhaling contaminated material | 2–5 days | Sudden death, discharge from eyes/nose, inability to stand | Pigs, ducks, geese | Sanitary segregation |
| **Fowl Spirochetosis** | Spirochete / Protozoa | Infective ticks | 1–2 days | Intense thirst, profuse diarrhea, gasping, sneezing | Duck and geese | Tick elimination |
| **Fowl Pneumonia** | Spores | Inhalation of spores from infected litter, food | - | Intense thirst, profuse diarrhea, gasping, sneezing | Duck and geese | Improved sanitation |
| **Fowl Pox** | Virus | Direct contact | 3–15 days | Rough, brown, wart-like sores on head, sore mouth and eyes | Poultry, turkeys, guinea fowls, ducks, geese | Vaccination twice yearly |
| **Avian Influenza (Zoonotic)** | Type A Viruses | Wild aquatic birds | 3–14 days | Swelling of head and wattle, bluish comb, dullness, respiratory discharge, bleeding in muscles and legs | Wild aquatic birds, poultry | Cannot be prevented with vaccine; cannot be treated successfully |
| **Classical Swine Fever (CSF)** | Pestivirus (Flaviviridae) | Virus transmission | 2–14 days | Inactive, drowsy, arched back, huddling, vomiting, high fever, anorexia, constipation, hair loss | Pigs | Modified Live Vaccines (MLV) |
| **Exotic Newcastle Disease (Ranikhet)** | Paramyxovirus (Type 5-6) | Contaminated water / direct contact | 2–15 days (avg) | Depression, diarrhea, respiratory lesions (10–50 mm diameter), lachrymation, nasal discharge | Poultry (does not affect humans) | Live virus vaccines (Lentogenic: F, B1; Mesogenic: H, R2B, Mukteshwar). Hygiene and sanitization. |
| **Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD)** | Lumpy Skin Disease Virus (LSDV) | Vector-borne pox disease | 2 to 5 weeks | Nodular skin disease | Cattle and water buffaloes | Vector control, sanitization, vaccination |
| **Haemorrhagic Septicemia (HS)** | *Pasteurella multocida* | Ingestion / Contact | 1–3 days | Fever, sudden drop in milk yield, abdominal pain, severe diarrhea, dysentery | Bovines | Annual vaccination |
| **Blue Tongue (BT)** | Arthropod-borne orbivirus (Reoviridae) | Rainy season vectors / Semen and placental route | - | Reddening and swelling of nose and oral mucosa, conjunctival congestion, dyspnea, snoring, pneumonia | Sheep and goats (rare in cattle; endemic in India) | Potassium permanganate wash (1g in 1L water, 2-3 times/day). Vaccine at 3 months, then annually. |
| **Peste-des-Petits Ruminants (PPR)** | Morbillivirus (Paramyxoviridae) | Direct contact / droplet | - | Fever, loss of appetite, stomatitis, gastroenteritis, pneumonitis (Evident mostly in goats; sheep less susceptible) | Goats and sheep | Vaccine at 3 months, then annually |

> [!WARNING]
> **EXAM TRAP (ICAR/JRF):** Anthrax carcasses **must never be opened** to prevent sporulation of *Bacillus anthracis* upon oxygen exposure. Disinfect strictly with 3% peracetic acid, 10% caustic soda, or 10% formalin.

> [!TIP]
> **AFO-2020 PAST QUESTION:** *Coccidiosis* is classically classified as a Protozoan parasitic infection affecting poultry and livestock. Dehydration during metabolic stress strongly correlates with electrolyte imbalances (Sodium, Potassium, Chlorine, Sulphur deficiencies).

## 4. Metabolic and Non-Infectious Disorders

* **Milk Fever:** Caused by acute low blood calcium levels (*hypocalcemia*) immediately following calving.
* **Ketosis:** Disturbance of carbohydrate metabolism in high-producing dairy cows, characterized by hypoglycemia and accumulation of ketone bodies, appearing from 7 days to 6 weeks post-calving.
* **Bloat (Tympany):** Over-distension of the rumen and reticulum with fermentation gases. Triggered by grazing lush, wet, young pastures containing legumes like clover, lucerne, and alfalfa, or any fast-growing plants.

## 6. Pharmacology: Common Veterinary Medicines & Uses

### Pharmacological Classifications
* **Antibiotics:** Fungal/bacterial metabolites inhibiting/killing microbes (e.g., Penicillin, Streptomycin, Chloramphenicol).
* **Antiseptics:** Non-cidal growth inhibitors for living tissues (e.g., Boric acid, Dettol, Iodine, Potassium Permanganate).
* **Disinfectants / Germicides:** Pathogen and spore destroyers for inanimate objects (e.g., Phenyl, Lime, Carbolic acid/Phenol, Lysol).
* **Purgatives & Laxatives:** Agents inducing defecation.
  * *Laxatives:* Soften stool (Magnesium Sulphate, Castor oil, Linseed oil, Sodium Sulphate).
  * *Drastic Purgatives:* Heavy evacuation agents (Barium chloride, Croton oil / *Crotonis oleum*).
* **Stimulants:** Excite bodily systems (Alcohol, Camphor, Caffeine).
* **Astringents:** Precipitate proteins to contract vessels/membranes; check bleeding/diarrhea (External: Tincture iodine, Alum; Internal: Catechu, Opium).
* **Massaging Oils:** Fatigue relief (Mustard oil, Turpentine oil, Sesame oil).
* **Antipyretics:** Lower fever (Aspirin, Quinine, Salicylic acid).
* **Anti-expectorants:** Suppress bronchial secretions (Opium, Belladonna).
* **Antidotes:** Counteract toxins (e.g., Iron salts for cyanide toxicity).
* **Anesthetics / Narcotics:** Induce unconsciousness/narcosis (Chloroform, Nitrous oxide, Ether, Chloral hydrate, Cannabis).
* **Caustics:** Burn and destroy tissues (Potassium permanganate, Copper sulphate, Zinc sulphate, Salt, Alum).
* **Emetics:** Induce vomiting (Copper sulphate, Zinc sulphate, Salt, Alum).
* **Anthelmintics:** Expel internal parasites (Finovice, Turpentine oil, Copper sulphate, Nicotine sulphate).
* **Deodorants:** Odor neutralizers (Phenyl, Bleaching powder).
* **Parasiticides:** External parasite destroyers (Methyl parathion, Phenyl, Copper sulphate).
* **Antacids (Carminatives):** Relieve gastric gases (Hing, Ajwain, Fenugreek, Fennel, Cumin).

---

### Specific Drug Concentrations & Clinical Doses
1. **Phenyl:** 1% solution used to wash hooves in FMD and sanitize animal housing.
2. **Carbolic Acid (Phenol):** 
   * 5% solution for cleaning cattle sheds.
   * 1% solution for washing wounds.
   * 2% solution for destroying external parasites.
3. **Potassium Permanganate ($\text{KMnO}_4$ / Red Drug):** 
   * 1% solution for sterilizing veterinary surgical instruments and hands.
   * 2% solution applied locally on snakebite areas.
4. **Lysol:** 2% solution for surgical instruments, cleaning, and uterine flushing.
5. **Magnesium Sulphate ($\text{MgSO}_4$ / Magsulf):** 
   * Large animals (Cow/Buffalo): 250–400 grams.
   * Small animals (Sheep/Goat): 50–100 grams.
6. **Castor Oil:** 
   * Large animals: 600–1200 grams.
   * Small animals: 50–125 grams.
7. **Alcohol:** 500 ml of 5% concentration (large animals) for fatigue and cold recovery.
8. **Copper Sulphate ($\text{CuSO}_4$ / Blue Stone):** 
   * 1% solution internal anti-parasitic drench: Large animals (250–300 ml), Small animals (50–70 ml).
   * 1% solution footbath for FMD hooves.
   * 2% solution spray on contaminated pastures to kill harmful insects.
9. **Alum:** 2–5% solution for washing wounds and uterine prolapse/inflammation in FMD.
10. **Turpentine Oil:** 30–60 ml mixed with base oils (internal); chest massage provides relief in pneumonia.

## 7. Swine Healthcare & Piglet Management

### Classical Swine Fever (CSF)
* **Causative Agent:** *Pestivirus* genus, *Flaviviridae* family.
* **Acute Symptoms:** Inactive, drowsy, arched back, droopy head, straight tail. Huddling, vomiting, high fever, anorexia, constipation, conjunctivitis, staggering gait, posterior weakness, and purple discoloration of abdominal skin. Late-stage recumbency, terminal convulsions, and severe watery diarrhea.
* **Chronic Symptoms:** Dullness, capricious appetite, pyrexia, diarrhea (up to 1 month), weight loss, hair loss, dermatitis, abdominal/ear discoloration, and disproportionately large head relative to trunk size.
* **Diagnosis & Control:** Field diagnosis via high morbidity/mortality, high fever, diarrhea, and kidney/lymph node petechial hemorrhages. **Control:** Modified Live Vaccines (MLV).

### Swine Influenza
* **Etiology:** Swine Influenza Virus (SIV).
* **Clinical Signs:** High fever ($40.5 - 41.5^\circ\text{C}$), puffy eyes, anorexia, weight loss, huddling, paroxysmal coughing, sneezing, irregular abdominal breathing, and ocular/nasal discharges. In breeding stock: abortions, infertility, small/weak litters, and increased stillbirths.
* **Control:** Strict biosecurity, "All-in/All-out" housing system, clean water, prevention of wild bird/duck contact, and terminal disinfection.

### Foot and Mouth Disease in Pigs
* **Etiology:** Aphthovirus.
* **Signs:** Fever ($40 - 40.6^\circ\text{C}$), anorexia, reluctance/screaming when forced to move. Vesicles on the coronary band, heels, interdigital space, and snout. *Mouth lesions are smaller, of shorter duration, and "dry-type" compared to cattle (no drooling).* Piglets may die suddenly without premonitory signs.

---

### Piglet Mortality & Neonatal Anemia
* **Mortality Rates:** Pre-weaning mortality ranges between **12–30%**.
* **Pre-partum Mortality:** Often linked to iron deficiencies in sows (corrected via iron injections).
* **Intra-partum Mortality:** Caused by hypoxia from interrupted placental blood flow during uterine contractions or premature umbilical cord rupture (predominant in aged sows).

#### Piglet Anemia (Iron Deficiency Anemia)
* **Pathophysiology:** Newborn piglets possess minimal hepatic iron reserves due to poor placental iron transfer. Sow's milk is notoriously low in iron.
* **Symptoms:** Pale ears and belly, rapid breathing ("thumps"), and secondary diarrhea.
* **Control & Prevention Protocols:**
  1. Placing fresh, clean earth daily in the pen.
  2. Soil drenching with a mix of **500 g ferrous sulphate**, **75 g copper sulphate**, and **3 liters of water**.
  3. Daily oral administration of **4 ml of 1.8% ferrous sulphate solution**.
  4. **Gold Standard (Safest & Easiest):** Intramuscular injection of **100–150 mg iron dextran** at **3 days of age**, followed optionally by a smaller booster at 3 weeks.
