## 1. Common Animal Husbandry Terminology & Definitions

* **Abattoir:** A slaughterhouse.
* **Adipose Tissue:** Fatty tissue of animals.
* **Ad Libitum (Ad Lib):** Term used to reflect the availability of a feed on a free-choice basis.
* **Agalactia:** Failure to secrete milk following parturition.
* **Anorexia:** Lack of appetite.
* **Balanced Ration:** A ration that contains all the nutrients in the right proportions and quantities.
* **Bovine:** Pertaining to / relating to cattle.
* **Ovine:** Pertaining to sheep.
* **Caprine:** Term relating to goats.
* **Equine:** Term relating to horses, mules, zebras, and asses.
* **Bull Calf:** A male calf under one year of age.
* **Bull:** Uncastrated sexually matured male of the bovine species.
* **Bullock:** Castrated male ox.
* **Calf:** A young animal of bovine species under one year of age.
* **Heifer Calf:** A female calf under one year of age.
* **Heifer:** A female individual (bovine) that has not yet calved.
* **Cow:** A female of bovine species that has calved at least once.
* **Steer:** Castrated male beef or dairy animal.
* **Calf Starter:** Concentrate feed offered to young calves after 2 weeks of age.
* **Calving:** The act of parturition in cattle.
* **Parturition:** Act of delivery in animals.
* **Casting:** Throwing down the animal and securing the limbs for various purposes like surgical operations, castration, hoof trimming, shearing, etc.
* **Castration:** Removal of testicles.
* **Challenge Feeding:** The practice of feeding higher levels of concentrate to challenge the cow to reach her maximum milk production.
* **Concentrates:** Feeds that contain less than 18% crude fiber (e.g., grains, oilcakes, grain by-products).
* **Roughage:** Feeds that contain more than 18% crude fiber (e.g., hay, silage, fodder) *(JRF)*.
* **Energy Feeds:** Feeds containing less than 20% crude protein.
* **Protein Supplements:** Feeds that contain 20% or more protein.
* **Ration:** The total amount of feed that an animal is offered during a 24-hour period.
* **Maintenance Ration:** A ration given daily to maintain an animal in a resting, non-production condition with good health.
* **Production Ration:** A portion of the ration given daily in excess of maintenance requirements for growth, production, and work.
* **Crisscrossing / Rotational Crossing:** Mating of a hybrid to three established breeds in a rotational manner *(AFO 2021)*.
* **Cross Breeding:** A system of breeding between two established breeds.
* **Line Breeding:** Mating of wider degree relations (not extremely close) resulting in uniformity *(AFO 2020)*.
* **Inbreeding:** A system of breeding between very closely related animals *(JRF)*.
* **Inbreeding Depression:** Reduced survival and fertility of offspring of related individuals.
* **Grading Up:** System of breeding in which pure bulls are used for improvement in non-descript females for several generations.
* **Test Cross:** Mating of a crossbred back to its recessive parent.
* **Culling:** Removal of undesirable or unproductive animals from the herd.
> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap:** Memorize the exact distinction between *Crisscrossing* (3-breed rotational mating) and *Grading Up* (using pure bulls on non-descript females over generations).
* **Conception:** Measure of a cow's fertility at service or the successful union of male and female in reproduction.
* **Deticking:** Removal of external parasites (ticks, lice, mites) present on the animal's body surface.
* **Deworming:** Removal of internal gastrointestinal parasites.
* **Dystocia:** Abnormal or difficult labor at parturition, causing difficulty in delivering the fetus and placenta.
* **Disbudding:** Removal of horn buds of a calf by mechanical or chemical methods to arrest horn growth.
* **Edema:** Presence of abnormally large amounts of fluid in intercellular tissue spaces (e.g., swelling of mammary glands accompanying parturition).
* **Fistula:** A permanent opening made in the body wall creating communication between the interior and external environment (e.g., rumen fistula).
* **Free Martin:** When twin calves of different sexes are born, the bull calf is normal whereas the heifer calf is sterile. The sterile female twin is called a free martin.
* **Gestation Period:** The period of pregnancy in animals.
* **Inter-calving Period:** Number of days between two successive calvings.
* **Involution:** The process where a pregnant cow's uterus returns to its pre-pregnancy state.
* **Lactation Curve:** Graphical representation of the rate of milk secretion during lactation.
* **Lactation Length:** Time interval between the date of calving to the date of drying the animal, expressed in days.
* **Dry Period:** Time interval between the date of drying off the cow and the date of next calving.
* **Milk Vein:** A large blood vessel running under the center of the animal's belly towards the udder.
* **Monoparous:** Animals that usually produce only one offspring at each pregnancy (horses and cattle).
* **Meconium:** The first feces, or stool, of the newborn.
* **Pasture:** Fodder crops grown on land for grazing animals.
* **Regurgitate:** To cast up undigested feed from the stomach to the mouth, as done by ruminants.
* **Scrub Bull / Scrub Animal:** A non-descript stray village bull or animal without a good progeny record.
* **Selection:** The process of including certain animals in a population to become parents of the next generation.
* **Service Period:** The period between parturition and successful conception, expressed in days.
* **Service:** The process in which a mature male covers a female.
* **Springer:** A female bovine showing signs of advanced pregnancy.
* **Standing Heat:** The period when female animals stand still when mounted by others (lasts 14–16 hours).
* **Silage:** Freshly cut green forages preserved by fermentation to the point of acidification (pH ~4.0), containing 65–70% moisture.
* **Ensiling:** The process of making silage in the absence of oxygen, where water-soluble carbohydrates ferment into organic acids.
* **Stud Bull:** A bull used specifically for breeding purposes.
* **Sternum:** The breastbone.
* **Tethering:** Controlling the movement of animals using a rope or chain.
* **Weaning:** Separation of the calf from the cow and feeding them artificially.
> [!NOTE]
> If weaning is practiced 4 days after calving, further rations must be fed strictly as per schedule.
* **Puberty:** Stage in females where ovaries are fully developed, egg formation begins, and the animal becomes capable of conceiving.
* **Estrus Cycle:** Physical events between two heating periods.
* **Estrus Period:** The state in which a female animal is willing to mate with a male.
* **Teaser Bull:** A vasectomized or altered bull used to identify females in the heating period.
* **Ovulation (Oviposition):** The exit of the mature ovum from the ovary/Graafian follicle.
* **Sire:** The male parent (father) of the calf.
* **Dam:** The female parent (mother) of the calf.
* **Hay:** Stored forage characterized by low moisture content (less than 16%).
* **Flushing:** Temporary, purposeful increase in nutrition/feed around breeding time to boost ovulation, conception, and embryo implantation rates by 10–20%.
* **Grooming:** Cleaning animals to keep coats free of dust, dirt, manure, and sweat.
* **Silent Heat (Sub-estrus):** Lack of behavioral estrus symptoms despite normal cyclical changes in genital organs (incidence: 10%–40% in herds).
* **Anestrous:** Period of sexual quiescence (dormancy) between two periods of sexual activity in cyclically breeding mammals.
* **Prolapsing of Uterus:** Condition where the uterus slips down into or protrudes out of the vagina.
* **Endometritis:** Mild, chronic infection of the uterus affecting up to 40% of post-calving cows, characterized by pus in the uterus and vulvular discharge while the cow continues to eat and cycle normally.
* **Colostrum:** The first mammary gland secretion released after parturition.
* **Lactogenesis:** The initiation of lactation (acquisition of milk-secreting ability by mammary glands).
* **Lactation:** The active secretion of milk from the mammary glands.

## 5. Ruminant Digestive System & Dentition

### Four-Chambered Stomach of Ruminants
1. **Rumen:** 
   * Largest compartment; acts as a microbial fermentation vat.
   * Temperature: **38–40°C** (ideal incubator environment) *(BHU)*.
   * Supports **rumination** (chewing cud). Ceasing rumination is an indicator of ill health.
2. **Reticulum:** 
   * Smaller sac with a thick honeycomb-patterned wall. Contents mix freely with the rumen. Connected to the omasum on the right side.
3. **Omasum:** 
   * Composed of muscular leaves with horny papillae resembling a book. Squeezes out water and fatty acids from ingesta passing through the reticulo-omasal orifice *(JRF)*.
4. **Abomasum:** 
   * The **true stomach** of the ruminant where enzymatic digestion proceeds identically to non-ruminants *(JRF)*.

---

### Cattle Dentition & Age Determination
* **Temporary (Deciduous / Milk) Teeth:** 20 total (8 incisors, 12 premolars, **0 molars**). Smaller and whiter than permanent teeth.
* **Permanent Teeth:** 32 total (8 incisors, 12 premolars, 12 molars) *(RRB SO 2019)*.
* Canine teeth are **absent** in ruminants. Age is determined solely using **incisor teeth**.

#### Eruption Schedule of Permanent Incisors in Cattle & Buffaloes
* **1st Pair:** 2 years 3 months
* **2nd Pair:** 3 years
* **3rd Pair:** 3 years 6 months
* **4th Pair:** 4 years

#### Alternative Age Estimation Methods
1. **General Appearance:** Shiny eyes, small/sharp horns, drawn skin, medium teeth, agile nature.
2. **Hooves:** Small and shiny in young animals.
3. **Horn Rings Formula:** $\text{Age} = 2 + \text{Number of rings on horn}$. 
   * *Note:* The first ring forms at 3 years of age, with one ring added annually. (Unreliable if cattle keepers file down rings).

## 7. Management Practices & Housing Systems

### Calf Management
* **Ear Tattooing:** Done at birth; permanent branding is done after **1 year** of age.
* **Dehorning:** Performed within **7–10 days** (3–10 days) of age using a red-hot iron, caustic potash sticks, or an electrical dehorning iron to destroy both horn buds.
* **Deworming:** Administered at **30-day intervals** (Use piperazine salts for ascaris prevention in calves up to 6 weeks).
* **Extra Teats:** Extra teats beyond 4 should be removed at **1–2 months** of age.
* **Castration of Males:** Performed at **8–9 weeks** of age.
* **Mineral Licks:** Provide mineral blocks to prevent mineral deficiencies and licking habits.
* **Colostrum Feeding:** Feed within **15 minutes** of calving at **1/10th of body weight** for cow calves, and **1/15th of body weight** for buffalo calves. Colostrum protein content is 4–5 times normal milk (Cow colostrum: **21% protein**; Buffalo colostrum: **23% protein**).
* **Daily Weight Gain:** Well-fed crossbred calves gain **400 grams/day** (2.5 to 3 kg/week) *(AFO 2018)*.
* **Calf Housing:** 
  * *0–3 months:* Individual housing.
  * *3–6 months:* Grouped housing.
  * *After 6 months:* Male and female calves housed separately.
  * *Mortality:* Highest in the first month due to pneumonia, diarrhea (calf scours), and worms.

---

### Housing Systems for Cattle
1. **Single Row Shed:** Maximum **12–16 animals** kept together. Slope is opposite the animal's mouth.
2. **Double Row Shed:** Used when animal count exceeds 16.
   * **Tail-to-Tail Method:** Best method for double row. Shed size = $31 \times 10.45\text{ m}^2$, max **28 animals**, includes a dedicated dung/urine drain and two manger spaces.
   * **Head-to-Head Method:** Shed size = $31 \times 10.36\text{ m}^2$, max **50 animals**, higher disease risk, raised center floor, one central manger, and two side drains.
3. **Stanchion Barn:** Housing system where feeding and milking stalls are combined.
4. **Floor Space Requirements:**
   * **Pregnant Cow:** **100–120 sq. feet** *(AFO 2018)*.

### Poultry Housing & Management
* **Brooding:** Application of artificial heat to chicks during early life stages using equipment called a **hover** *(RRB SO 2021)*.
* **Housing Systems:** 
  * **Deep Litter System:** Litter material (sawdust, rice husk) accumulates with manure until reaching a depth of **8–12 inches** *(RRB SO 2021)*.
  * **Battery Cage System:** Commercial layer housing using cages with dimensions **$14 \times 16\text{ inches}$** and a height of **17 inches** *(AFO 2021)*.
* **Space Requirements:** 
  * Layers / Hens: **1 sq. ft.** per bird *(AFO 2017)*.
  * Broiler Poultry: **1 sq. ft.** per bird *(AFO 2018)*.
* **Production Parameters:**
  * Broiler Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR): **1.5 to 1.9** *(AFO 2020)*.
  * Broiler Dressing Percentage: **70–75%** *(TNPSC 2023)*.
  * Number of broiler batches reared per year: **7** (Ideal system maintains 5–6 batches concurrently) *(AFO 2018)*.

## 8. High-Yield Facts, Exam Trivia & Disease Profiles

### Milk & Dairy Chemistry
* **Milk Emulsion Type:** Oil in water emulsion *(AFO 2020)*.
* **Specific Gravities & Standards (FSSAI):**
  * Cow Milk SNF: **8.5%** *(AFO 2021)*. Water percentage in cow milk is ~**87%**.
  * Cow Milk Fat / Color: A2 milk (desi cow) vs A1 milk (exotic cow). Histidine is present at the **67th position** in the molecular chain of A1 milk but is absent in A2 milk *(RRB SO 2021)*.
  * Yellow color of cow milk is due to **Carotenoid / Carotene** *(AFO 2020, RRB SO 2019)*.
  * Buffalo milk is whiter because **beta-carotene is converted into colorless vitamin A** in buffaloes *(AFO 2021)*.
* **Fat & Protein Extremes:**
  * Highest fat in exotic cattle: **Jersey** (> 5%).
  * Highest fat in buffaloes: **Bhadawari** breed.
  * Highest protein content across livestock: **Sheep**.
* **Processing & Testing:**
  * Homogenization: Breaking large fat globules into smaller particles *(AFO 2021)*.
  * Pasteurization Purpose: To destroy pathogenic microorganisms/disease-causing bacteria.
  * Alkaline Test: Used to identify incomplete pasteurization.
  * Reichert-Meissl (R.M.) Test: Used to test **ghee purity** *(AFO 2021)*.

---

### Disease Classifications & Pathological Agents
* **Tuberculosis:** Caused by *Mycobacterium bovis*. Characterized by dry cough followed by wet cough. **Goats** possess natural resistance to tuberculosis.
* **Foot-and-Mouth Disease (FMD):** Highly infectious viral disease attacking cloven-footed animals. Symptoms include drooling of saliva, ulcers in gums/lips, and high mortality in suckling calves. Sheep are vaccinated against FMD every **6 months** *(AFO 2020)*.
* **Black Quarter (BQ):** Pathognomonic symptom is a **crepitating sound** on pressing the affected muscle tissue *(MP PAT 2021)*.
* **Tympanitis (Bloat / Afra):** Caused by excessive feeding of lush green/lush succulent forages, leading to ruminal gas accumulation treated via trocar and canula *(AAO 2019)*.
* **Milk Fever (Parturient Paresis):** Caused by **calcium deficiency** around parturition; no vaccine exists yet *(RRB SO 2019)*.
* **Bluetongue Disease:** Non-contagious, insect-borne viral disease of ruminants (primarily sheep; less frequently cattle, goats, buffaloes). Not a bacterial disease *(RRB SO 2019)*.
* **Japanese Encephalitis:** **Pigs** serve as the primary amplifier host *(AFO 2021)*.
* **Diamond Skin Disease:** Associated with **pigs** *(BHU 2017)*.
* **Pink Eye in Bovines:** Caused by *Moraxella bovis* *(TNPSC VAS 2020)*.
* **Surra Disease:** Incubation period is **5–30 days** *(RRB SO 2019)*.
* **Nutritional Deficiency Disorders:**
  * Rickets & Osteomalacia: **Ca / P deficiency**
  * Anemia: **Fe deficiency**
  * Pica: **P deficiency**
  * Wilson's Disease: **Cu deficiency**
  * Grass Tetany: **Mg deficiency / K toxicity**
  * Curled Toe Paralysis (Chicken): **Vitamin B2 deficiency**
  * Crazy Chick Disease: **Vitamin E deficiency**
  * Cannibalism (Poultry): **NaCl deficiency**

### Miscellaneous High-Yield Exam Facts
* **Insurance Premium:** Standard livestock insurance premium is **5%** of the animal's cost *(TNPSC VAS 2020)*.
* **Bullock Power:** A full-grown pure Indian draft bullock power rating is **0.70 HP** (average is **0.5 HP**). Cultivates **0.33 Ha** of land. A 35 HP tractor ploughs ~2.5 Ha in 8 hours consuming 5 L diesel/hr.
* **Wool Production in India:** Maximum in **Rajasthan (35%)**.
* **Angora Goat Wool:** Known as **Mohair**.
* **Bacterial Fermentation in Milk:**
  * Milk carbohydrate fermentation: *Streptococcus lactis*.
  * Milk-to-curd conversion: *Lactobacillus thermophilus*.
  * Yoghurt: Coagulated product via *Lactobacillus bulgaricus* and *Streptococcus thermophilus*.
* **Specific Breed Trivia:**
  * Kadaknath: Native to Dhar district, M.P.; produces black-colored meat *(RRB SO 2021)*.
  * Surti Buffalo: Characterized by **sickle-shaped horns** and greyish-brown/black coats with two white collars (one around jaw, one around brisket) *(AFO 2021, TNPSC VAS 2020)*.
  * Pulikulam: Famous for **Jallikattu** in Southern India *(TNPSC VAS 2020)*.
  * Saanen: Swiss goat breed known as the **"Milk Queen of the World"** *(AFO 2022)*.
  * Aseel: Hen breed known for fighting sports and high 5-week body weight *(AFO 2018)*.
  * Chokla: Indian sheep breed producing superior quality carpet wool; hornless *(AFO 2016)*.
  * Hisardale: Crossbreed of Bikaneri ewes and Merino rams *(RRB SO 2019)*.
  * Godavari: Buffalo breed evolved by grading up local coastal Andhra Pradesh buffaloes with Murrah *(RRB SO 2021)*.
