# Dairy Science & Processing Technology

## 1. Fundamentals of Milk and Physical Properties

Milk is defined as the entire lacteal secretion of the mammary glands of mammals obtained by the process of milking during the period following at least **72 hours** after calving or until milk is free from colostrum.

### Physical Properties of Milk
* **Acidity and pH:** 0.14% to 0.18%, pH 6.5 to 6.7 *(JRF Exam Trap)*
* **Specific Gravity:** 1.018 to 1.036
* **Density:** 1.027 to 1.033 $\text{kg/cm}^3$
* **Viscosity:** 2.0 cp (centipoise). *Note:* Whole milk possesses the highest viscosity (2.0 cp), skim milk possesses medium viscosity (1.5 cp), and whey possesses the lowest viscosity (1.2 cp).
* **Surface Tension:** 50 dyne/cm
* **Freezing Point:** $-0.575^\circ\text{C}$
* **Boiling Point:** $100.17^\circ\text{C}$
* **Total Calories:** 79.02 kcal per 100 ml of milk.
* **Nitrogen Content in Milk:** 0.5%

> [!WARNING]
> **JRF/AFO Exam Trap:** Pay close attention to pH (6.5–6.7) and Acidity (0.14–0.18%). Do not confuse specific gravity (1.018–1.036) with density (1.027–1.033 $\text{kg/cm}^3$).

## 5. Milking Methods and Practices

* **Milking Definition:** A critical, laborious process involving hormonal reflexes. 
* **Duration:** The art of milking is performed within **5 to 8 minutes**.
* **Frequency:** Normally done twice a day. Animals yielding up to **10 liters** should be milked twice a day; frequency increases to **thrice** for production ranging from **12–15 liters**.
* **Milking Methods:**
  1. **Fisting / Full Hand Method:** Best method of hand milking, though most milkmen incorrectly use knuckling. *(BHU, Pre-PG)*
  2. **Knuckling Method:** Milkers bend their thumb against the teat canal and drag milk out. **Avoid this practice** as it is injurious to teats.
  3. **Stripping:** Practiced where the teat length is small; normally applied towards the end of milking to evacuate milk completely.
  4. **Machine Milking:** Replicates calf suckling. One milking machine for **10 animals yielding 10 liters/day** is economical to maintain.

## 6. Milk Processing Technologies

### Homogenization
* **Objective:** Prevents creaming or the rising of fat to the top of containers by breaking fat particles into smaller particles. *(AFO-2021)*

### Pasteurization
* **Objective:** Minimizes health hazards from pathogenic microorganisms by killing **99%** of microorganisms via heat treatment. Milk is immediately cooled to **$10^\circ\text{C}$** post-treatment.

| Pasteurization Type | Temperature | Time |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Batch Pasteurization (LTLT) / Holding Method** *(JRF)* | $63^\circ\text{C}$ | 30 minutes |
| **High Temperature Short Time (HTST) / Continuous / Flash** | $72^\circ\text{C}$ | 15 seconds |
| **Ultra-High Temperature (UHT) Sterilization** | $135^\circ\text{C} - 145^\circ\text{C}$ | 1–5 seconds |

### Thermization
* Heating milk to **$63–65^\circ\text{C}$ for 15 seconds** and rapidly chilling to **$4^\circ\text{C}$**.

## 8. Milk Testing and Adulteration Detection

| Test Name | Used For / Objective |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Clot on Boiling Test (COB)** | Stability of milk for heat processing |
| **Alcohol Test** | Detect abnormal milk (colostrum or mastitis milk) |
| **Sediment Test** | Identify dust/dirt in milk |
| **Resazurin Reduction Test (RRT)** | Bacteriological quality of milk (dye reduction/detection) |
| **Lactometer Test** | Determination of adulteration by adding water |
| **Methylene Blue Reduction Test (MBRT)** | Bacteriological quality / microbial load (MP-PAT) |
| **Alcohol-Alizarin Test** | Check suitability of milk for high heat treatment and acidity |
| **Alkaline Phosphatase Test** | Check adequacy of pasteurization (Dairy farming) |
| **Hansa Test** | Detect adulteration of cow milk with buffalo milk |
| **Iodine Test** | Detect starch content in milk |
| **Nitrate Test** | Detect rainwater content in milk |
| **California Mastitis Test (Mested Solution Test)** | Detect mastitis disease |

### Chemical Detection Tests
* **Rosalic Acid Test (Neutralizers):** Add 4 drops of freshly prepared alcoholic solution of 1% Rosalic acid.
  * *Pink colour:* Presence of sodium hydroxide, potassium hydroxide, or calcium hydroxide.
  * *Rose red colour:* Presence of sodium carbonate or sodium bicarbonate.
  * *Brownish colour:* Absence of any neutralizers.
* **Urea Detection:** Take 2 ml of milk + 2 ml of *p*-dimethylaminobenzaldehyde reagent.
  * *Distinct yellow colour:* Denotes presence of urea.

## 9. High-Yield Dairy Facts & Trivia

* **Global Standing:** India holds the \#1 position in annual milk production with the highest annual growth rate of **5–6%**.
* **Per Capita Availability:** Increased from 252 g/day (2007–08) to **394 g/day** (2018–19). Highest among states is in **Punjab**.
* **State Production Ranking:** Uttar Pradesh (Highest) followed by Rajasthan.
* **Global Cow Milk:** India is the 2nd largest producer of cow milk followed by the USA.
* **White Revolution (Operation Flood):** Launched in **1970** by **Dr. Verghese Kurien** (*Father of the White Revolution / Milkman of India*). *(RRB SO-2021)*
* **National Milk Day:** Celebrated every year on **November 26** (since 2014) to commemorate Dr. Verghese Kurien's birth anniversary.
* **Animal Behavior:** 
  * Cows spend **6–7 hours** a day eating and chew at least **50 times per minute**.
  * A person can milk **6 cows by hand in 1 hour**, or **100 cows in 1 hour** using milking machines.
  * Talking, singing, or whistling to livestock during milking relaxes them and aids milk let-down.
  * Wallowing is a common behavioral trait of **buffaloes**.
* **Nutritional Highlights:**
  * Milk is a poor source of iron and vitamin C, but a rare food source of **Vitamin D**, containing phosphorus, potassium, and **Vitamin K2**.
  * Sweet taste of milk is due to **lactose**. Humans digest it via **lactase**.
  * Lactose is used as a coating/filler material in the pharmaceutical industry for pills/tablets.
  * Casein cleanses burning taste buds after eating spicy food.
* **Pigmentation & Color:**
  * **Cow milk yellow colour:** Due to **beta-carotene** from grass. The body converts beta-carotene into **Vitamin A** upon ingestion. *(AFO-2020)*
  * **Buffalo milk white colour:** Beta-carotene is already converted into Vitamin A inside the animal's body.
  * **White milk color:** Due to **casein** protein.
* **Hormonal Control:**
  * **Oxytocin:** Responsible for milk let-down. *(AFO-2020)*
  * **Adrenaline:** Responsible for stopping lactation. *(RRB SO-2021)*
  * **Prolactin:** Responsible for milk production.
* **Key Livestock & Breed Facts:**
  * Highest body weight in Indian cow: **Kankrej**
  * Highest fat percent in exotic cow breeds: **Jersey**
  * Highest milk fat percent in buffalo: **Bhadawri (8–13%)**
  * Highest milk producing buffalo breed / Best buffalo breed: **Murrah**
  * Respiration rate of buffalo: **15–20 per minute**
  * Heat period of cow and buffalo: **18–36 hours**
  * Estrus appearance post-calving: **30–40 days**
  * Minimum dry period for a cow: **40 days**
  * Goat title: **Poor man's cow**
  * Saanen title: **Milk queen of the goat world**
  * Pig meat: Salted smoked meat of pig is **Bacon**.
  * Livestock insurance scheme started: **2005–06**
  * Testosterone: **Male hormone**
  * Rinderpest: **Cattle plague**
  * Milk Fever: Caused by **Calcium (Ca)** deficiency. *(AFO-2016)*
* **Cheese & Curd Biochemistry:**
  * Cheese is made of **casein** by adding **rennet** to milk.
  * Rennet separates milk into solid curds and liquid whey.
  * Best temperature of milk for adding starter to prepare curd: **$21^\circ\text{C}$**.
  * Fat is present as an **emulsion** (micro-globules known as triglycerides).
  * Protein is present in a **colloidal** state.
  * Major milk protein: **Casein (80%)** (Cow milk: 3.0%, Buffalo milk: 4.3%).
  * Lactose content: Cow milk (4.5%), Buffalo milk (4.8%).
  * Kjeldahl method: Used to detect milk protein.
* **Microbial Action:**
  * Curdling without pronounced acid production (**Sweet curdling**): Caused by *Bacillus mycoides* & *Bacillus cereus*.
  * Recknagel phenomenon: Density/specific gravity of fresh milk is lower than milk held for long periods (especially under cold storage).
  * Carbohydrate bacterial fermentation: *Streptococcus lactis*
  * Fat bacterial formation: *Pseudomonas fragi*
  * Milk-to-curd conversion: *Lactobacillus thermophilus*
