# Animal Feeds, Fodder & Nutrition

## 1. Digestive System of Cattle (Ruminants)

The digestive organs of ruminants consist of the mouth, esophagus, rumen, reticulum, omasum, abomasum, small intestine, and large intestine. 

* **Mouth & Esophagus:** The cow takes food into the mouth. After mixing with saliva, the food enters the rumen through the esophagus.
* **Cattle Stomach (Four Compartments):**

> [!WARNING]
> **Exam Trap (BHU / JRF):** Questions frequently target specific compartment functions, temperature ranges, and anatomical descriptions (e.g., "honeycomb" structure or "true stomach"). Ensure absolute clarity on structural terminology.

1. **Rumen:** 
   * **Function:** The ruminant chews grass and swallows it into the rumen. When finished eating, the food is brought back up and re-chewed (**chewing cud** or **rumination**). Stopping rumination is a direct sign of ill health.
   * **Microbial Action:** The feed is subjected to extensive microbial fermentation. 
   * **Anatomy:** This enlarged compartment is the biggest of the four-chambered stomach.
   * **Temperature:** **38–40°C**, creating an ideal natural incubator for micro-organisms to flourish. *(BHU)*

2. **Reticulum:** 
   * **Anatomy:** A smaller sac partially separated from the rumen with a thick wall resembling a **honeycomb**. 
   * **Mixing:** The contents of the rumen and reticulum mix freely. 
   * **Connection:** Connected to the omasum (the third compartment) through an opening on the right side of the reticulum.

3. **Omasum:** 
   * **Anatomy:** Contains many layers of muscular leaves resembling a book. 
   * **Function:** After fermentation, the ingesta passes through the **reticulo-omasal orifice** into the omasum. The muscular leaves are equipped with **horny papillae** that squeeze out the ingesta. Water and fatty acids so squeezed out are absorbed here. *(JRF)*

4. **Abomasum:** 
   * **Status:** The **true stomach** in the ruminant. 
   * **Digestion:** From this organ onwards, digestion in ruminants proceeds identically to that of non-ruminants. *(JRF)*

## 5. Nutrient Content in Animal & Poultry Manure

### Comparative Ranking Matrices

* **Nitrogen (N):** 
  * Highest: Poultry > Sheep > Pig
  * Lowest: Horse > Cattle
  * Relative Ratio: Sheep ($\text{N}>\text{K}$), Horse ($\text{N}>\text{K}$), Poultry ($\text{N}>\text{K}$)
* **Phosphorus (P):** 
  * Highest: Poultry > Pig
  * Lowest: Horse > Cattle
* **Potassium (K):** 
  * Highest: Pig > Sheep > Poultry
  * Lowest: Horse > Sheep
  * Relative Ratio: Pig ($\text{K}>\text{N}$)
* **Calcium (Ca):** 
  * Highest: Poultry > Horse
  * Lowest: Cattle > Sheep
* **Magnesium (Mg):** 
  * Highest: Cattle > Poultry
  * Lowest: Pig > Horse
* **Sulfur (S):** 
  * Highest: Poultry > Pig
  * Lowest: Horse > Cattle

> [!WARNING]
> **AFO-2018 Exam Focus:** 
> * **N, P, Ca, S** are **Highest in Poultry**.
> * **K** is **Highest in Pig**.
> * **Mg** is **Highest in Cattle** and **Lowest in Pig**.

## 6. Water Requirements

* **Non-stressed Cattle:** Non-heat stressed non-lactating cattle consume **5–6% of body weight per day**.
* **Heat Stress:** Water consumption increases by **12% or more** of body weight per day during heat stress.
* **Feed-to-Water Ratio:** Animals consume **2 to 5 kg of water for every 1 kg of dry feed** consumed under non-heat-stressed conditions.

### Daily Water Requirements by Species

| Animal Species / Category | Water Consumption (Litres/Day) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Dairy cattle** | 70–110 |
| **Beef cattle** | 22–66 |
| **Sheep and goat** | 4–5 |
| **Chickens** | 0.2–0.4 |

## 7. Feeding Management (Swine / Pigs)

### A. Boars & Sows
* **Boars:** A breeding boar requires **2–2.5 kg concentrate per 100 kg weight** based on age, condition, and breeding demand.
* **Female Sows / Gilts:** 
  * Mature sows gain **30–35 kg** and gilts **40–45 kg** during pregnancy. 
  * Rations must prevent them from becoming over-fat or thin. **Individual feeding is strongly preferred.**
* **Flushing:** The practice of supplying extra feed to sows and gilts from **1–2 weeks prior to mating**, returning to normal feeding immediately after mating.

### B. Farrowing Sow, Litter & Creep Feeding
* **Farrowing Protocol:** Feed lightly with **bulky laxative feed** immediately before and after farrowing. Bring the sow to full feeding over **10 days**.
* **Feed Calculation:** 
  $$\text{Feed Allowance} = 2.5–3\text{ kg} / 100\text{ kg body weight} + 0.2\text{ kg feed per piglet}$$
  * *Example:* A 100 kg sow with 8 piglets receives: 
    $$3\text{ kg (sow)} + (8 \times 0.2\text{ kg} = 1.6\text{ kg}) = \mathbf{4.6\text{ kg feed/day}}$$
* **Creep Feeding:** 
  * Definition: The practice of **self-feeding concentrates to young piglets** in a separate enclosure away from the mother.
  * Initiation: Provided when piglets are **two weeks old** (fed from **14 to 56 days**).
  * Intake & Growth: An active, healthy piglet consumes about **10 kg of feed** before reaching 8 weeks of age, with **two-thirds** of this consumed between **6–8 weeks**.
  * Nutrient Density: Also called **pig starter**. Sows' milk alone cannot fulfill maximum growth requirements; creep feed contains **25–30% Crude Protein (CP)**.
  * Setup: A "creep" is a protected physical device or pen corner allowing only piglets access.
